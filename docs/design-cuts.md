# Camera cuts: spend less on the first view without borrowing the old view

Study against main **`73eee447f0617ef410fa4e1c45220196a808b49c`**, after release **v0.1.3 / `ebfc21b`**. Reading and reasoning only; no renderer changes, builds, captures or GPU experiments.

**Recommendation:** screen a smaller, uniformly sampled cold sun budget first, with full receiver coverage and the existing reconstruction. Preserve the valid world caches, as the engine already does. If the reduced budget produces visible grain or missing penumbra detail, switch to prefetch for announced cuts; do not conceal the loss with old-view history or a larger blur. Budgeted GI refill is a separate, conditional follow-up, requiring a valid fallback before any cap.

Number labels throughout: **M** = measured in the linked repository report, not measured by this study; **R** = reported by a named primary source (including repository code constants and the coordination brief, explicitly distinguished from timings); **E** = this study's estimate or proposed experiment parameter. Versions, patent/section identifiers, dates, hashes and source line numbers are identifiers, not performance measurements. Arithmetic derived from M inputs is labelled E; it predicts neither a measured speedup nor visual acceptance.

## The gap and the evidence to preserve

**M — [P-YARD2](p-yard.md):** matched quarry, native **1920×1080**, RTX **4070 SUPER**, UE **5.7.4**, Lumen + TSR Epic; GPU frame times. Cut observations are the median of the single cut in each of **two processes**, not a repeated-cut distribution.

| M state | Renderlab RT | UE HW Epic | Renderlab non-RT | UE SW Epic |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Still, ms | 3.82 | 4.50 | 8.65 | 4.02 |
| Moving, ms | 4.26 | 4.57 | 10.57 | 4.12 |
| Cut, ms | 12.60 | 4.52 | 43.11 | 4.11 |
| Recovery window, ms | 3.53 | 4.48 | 8.71 | 4.04 |

**E arithmetic:** the cut excess over UE is `12.60 − 4.52 = 8.08 ms` RT and `43.11 − 4.11 = 39.00 ms` non-RT. The excess over Renderlab's own recovery is **E 9.07 / 34.40 ms**. These are targets to explain, not proven attribution to shadows. **M — [P-SHOW2](p-show2.md#quarry-rows-root-steer-ratio-against-unreal-epic)** subsequently measured **12.70 / 43.34 ms** cuts on its head in a single visit per path: the problem survives the release work.

The earlier results rule out several tempting restarts of exploration:

- **M — [P-S1](p-s1.md), [P-S3Q](p-s3q.md), [gaps](m7-gaps.md):** umbra pixels contain only **2.8%** of game-cut lookup work; replayable shared walks only **1.2%**. Quarry replayability was **34%**, yet exact replay made the cut **9.22 → 11.00 ms**. Do not spend this unit rebuilding certificates or the rejected replay.
- **M — [P-RTS](p-rts.md):** probe-listed RT game cuts improved CPU service **21.6 → 5.6 ms**, but missed penumbra fringes. In the wide-sun screen, the final classifier missed **122** cold-frame pixels with visibility differences **0.004–0.047**, and moving losses reached **0.125**. Listing remains disabled. A classifier that declares an unsampled receiver fully lit/blocked is a different and riskier approximation than uniformly sampling every receiver.
- **M — [P-DEFECT](p-defect.md), [P-SHOW2 §2](p-show2.md#2-regression-on-main-and-the-relight-switch-step-2-merge):** the old non-RT GI gather cap of **8,192 cells** caused long-lived cut blotches; **32,768** drained cleanly. P-SHOW2 already removed the false held-skin relight. Neither the relight deletion nor returning to the smaller gather budget is a new cut optimization.
- **R — coordination brief:** cut timings can swing **5–13×** between processes until warmed. I did not locate a retained paired table establishing that exact range in the checked-out reports. [P-NRT](p-nrt.md) independently warns that its single first-cut sample swings by several milliseconds. Separate first-use resource/pipeline warm-up from a warmed camera cut, and preserve the outliers rather than treating them as algorithmic savings.

## What the source actually restarts

[`Engine::cut()`](../src/engine/mod.rs#L1033-L1041) calls `cut_reconstruction()`, `reset_atmosphere_history()` and `mark_camera_cut()`. It **does not call `reset_indirect()`**, rebuild acceleration structures or globally flush virtual shadows. A camera can nevertheless reveal new geometry, texture levels, shadow cells and GI keys. That conditional work is important, but is not evidence of a global cut invalidator.

The following inventory covers the normal engine cut on both first-class paths. A renderer caller bypassing `Engine` must also reset fog; `cut_reconstruction()` alone is narrower by design.

## What Unreal amortizes, and what its cut sequence gives up

**TSR:** Epic's [UE 5.7 TSR documentation, Spatial Anti-Aliaser](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/temporal-super-resolution-in-unreal-engine?application_version=5.7#spatialanti-aliaser) explicitly identifies a camera cut as a case with only current-frame data. An FXAA-style spatial fallback hides aliasing while details accumulate; it cannot recreate absent detail. The first frames can therefore be softer and less detailed than the converged view. **R source example:** at **50%** input fraction and **60 Hz**, its acquisition formula `1000 / (fraction² × fps)` gives about **66.6 ms**, or **4 frames**, for a sample per display pixel. That is neither full convergence time nor GPU cost, and the matched native-resolution yardstick must not be assigned this lower-resolution example's recovery duration.

**Lumen:** Epic's [UE 5.7 technical details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine?application_version=5.7) explain that Surface Cache lighting updates are amortized over frames and scene captures are throttled. Fast movement can outrun updates and make indirect light pop in; lighting changes propagate gradually. Missing Surface Cache coverage can produce black reflections. This supports a risk of an initially dark or incomplete indirect/reflected contribution in an uncached region, **not** a claim that every warm camera cut necessarily starts dark.

[Wright et al., SIGGRAPH 2022 Lumen course notes](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-Lumen-Wright%20et%20al.pdf), “Update Strategy,” “Caching” and “Lighting Latency,” describe persistent world probes, tracing newly revealed probe positions, subset refresh and fixed card-update budgets; previous lighting is reused where available. **R historical talk budgets:** capture **512²**, direct lighting **1024²**, indirect lighting **512² texels per frame**. These are the talk's algorithm figures, not verified UE 5.7 cut budgets. The [UE 5.7 performance guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine?application_version=5.7) warns that too-small radiance-cache probe budgets cause lighting pops during fast motion. Bounded work buys latency stability by accepting lighting latency; Renderlab should borrow the scheduling structure only when its fallback passes the visible-sequence bar.

Epic's [cinematic temporal-artifact guidance](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/cinematic-workflow-tips-for-sequencer-in-unreal-engine) describes hard edges and sparkling/grainy reflective surfaces before history builds. Its [Movie Render Queue warm-up settings](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/cinematic-rendering-image-quality-settings-in-unreal-engine) rebuild temporal history before recording a shot. These currently resolve to newer documentation than the matched version; they are shot-start/offline guidance, not a gameplay policy of secretly rendering warm-up frames after every cut.

**Virtual shadow maps:** Epic's [VSM caching guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/ru-ru/unreal-engine/virtual-shadow-maps-in-unreal-engine) describes depth-buffer-driven requests and persistent shadow pages, with redraw for newly exposed or invalidated pages. It explicitly warns about camera cuts, fast motion and large disocclusions: a cut is not automatically free because pages are cached. The retrieved page is labelled UE 5.8, so this is algorithm context rather than verification of the matched version's exact cut policy. Unlike Lumen's amortized lighting updates, this source does not establish a general policy of deferring required shadow pages after a cut; do not claim UE deliberately renders a temporarily unshadowed first frame.

Public primary material does not settle the exact UE gameplay-cut invalidation list, cut-specific budget multipliers or a universal darkness duration. P-YARD's warm same-region cut retained Lumen world state; it already shows a relatively cheap UE cut without certifying distant cold-cut behavior. Separate warm angle cut, warm return, distant uncached cut, cold startup and cut plus lighting change. Do not excuse new Renderlab blotches because some other Lumen sequence can pop.

## Ranked changes and their rejection screens

The ranking is **E judgement of saving per visible risk**, conditioned on the current source. The estimates overlap: use a new pass ledger after each retained change, never add all forecast savings. Preserve the native primary visibility, material interfaces and explicit view-history reset. None requires screen-space transport or replacing a hit's shader with a fixed lighting model.

### First: smaller uniform cold sun budget, then fresh samples on later frames

Use **E trial 64** cold samples per receiver rather than **R code 256**, retaining the full sun disk and every receiver. Let the existing rotating fresh samples and TR1 refine subsequent frames; do not classify a receiver clear from a few probes and do not carry pre-cut history.

**E saving:** the old RT quarry slope predicts `(256 − 64) × 0.044 = 8.45 ms` less fresh-primary/shadow work. Applied only as a sizing scenario to **M 12.60 ms**, this would leave **E 4.15 ms**, near the UE **M 4.52 ms** cut; current profiling must verify the slope and floor. For the software march, assume **E 22–28 ms** of the historical **M 32.8 ms** cut sun pass scales with direction count: `W × (1 − 64/256) = 16.5–21.0 ms` saved, predicting **E 22.1–26.6 ms** from the **M 43.11 ms** total. That would be useful, but far from UE. Page/ring setup and newly seen GI remain.

For selected S4 scenes, do not promise a proportional saving: **M P-S4C** decomposes game cut **9.07 ms** into traversal/classification **5.08** plus direction tests **3.99**. An optimistic **E** direction-only model saves `3.99 × 3/4 = 2.99 ms`, leaving **E 6.08 ms** sun work. The report cautions that the selector's restart performance was not separately measured; actual loop/cull behavior and selector changes can erase this forecast.

**Visible risk:** first-frame penumbra grain, bright contact specks and reflection noise becoming more exposed as other lighting settles. The reflection estimator is unchanged, so this is not a firefly fix. Repeated cuts can continually prevent convergence. A proposed **E 4-frame** catch-up plan of sun counts `64,64,64,64` totals **E 256**, while current `256,32,32,32` totals **R code-derived 352** on an otherwise active track; equal totals still do not guarantee equal pixel coverage or image quality. Screen the simple cold-only change first; add a catch-up schedule only if needed, and judge every recovery frame.

**Smallest screen, E:** one loaded quarry, both backends in one **10-minute** lease, no reference regeneration; baseline / cold-64 / baseline cuts alternating after the same warmed path. Hold the destination briefly, then immediately move; also repeat cuts without a settling hold. Save raw lighting and final images at **E frames 0,1,2,4,8,16,32**, plus a normal-speed clip and per-frame pass times. **Pass:** lower warmed cut and sequence peak, no new grain, contact specks, crawling fringe, smear or brightness pulse at display scale against baseline and saved UE sequence. **Fail:** thin penumbra lost, bright edge dots increase, held destination visibly refines in distracting steps, or recovery peak merely moves later. Prefer a failed screen to adding blur. If it passes quarry, promotion still needs the wide-sun/animated game witness where P-RTS failed; a single-scene rejection screen is not certification.

### Second: prefetch an announced destination without advancing view history

Add an optional engine announcement of the next cut's camera and scene epoch, **E one frame ahead**. Prepare only destination cache requests/resources—GI keys and their dependencies, shadow pages/receiver cells and texture levels—into versioned world state, while the displayed frame keeps its own camera, TR1, fog and temporal sample state.

This is new API work; `Engine::cut()` currently announces only the current cut. It is useful for scripted cameras/teleports with known destinations, not a substitute for a cheap unannounced cut. Keep all useful world entries already retained; invalidating them less cannot save work that is not invalidated today. If a spare prefetch submission would race active cache writers, serialize it or use a request staging buffer, not a hidden full render that mutates history.

**E saving:** let the measured cut's destination-only refill cost be `P`, and actual previous-frame slack be `S`; cut reduction is at most `min(P,S) − scheduling overhead`. Illustrative **E** `P = 4 ms RT / 8 ms non-RT`, `S = 2 ms`, overhead **0.1–0.3 ms** gives **E 1.7–1.9 ms** cut reduction on either path, with that **E 2 ms** added before the cut. With no slack, legitimate sequence-peak saving is **E zero**, even if the cut timestamp falls. It does not eliminate the fresh full-screen sun rays of candidate one. No measured P-YARD prefetch split exists.

**Visible risk:** stale prefetched light/geometry if an actor or sun changes between announcement and use; cache eviction of the displayed view; wasted work when the cut is cancelled. Epoch validation must reject stale entries; fallback uses ordinary current work. Never prefill TR1/fog from a camera that was not displayed and never preserve a pre-cut pixel to mask incompleteness.

**Smallest screen, E:** one quarry, **12-minute** lease, warm destination-return cut with announced and unannounced controls; run the pre-cut camera motion too. A final repeated trial changes a caster/light after announcement. **Pass:** destination request misses decrease, no stale shadow/lighting/fog, no extra pop in the old view and lower maximum presented-frame latency across the pre-cut/cut/recovery sequence. **Fail:** the hitch shifts to the preceding frame, state advances the jitter sequence, or moving geometry leaves old illumination. If application scheduling has no slack or advance notice, reject this as the general solution before implementation; retain it only as an optional API opportunity.

### Third: bounded newly seen GI work with a valid world fallback

Keep the valid table, but spread the **newly requested** work from a cut over fixed slot groups under a bounded budget, instead of confusing it with the existing post-clear `cut_phases`. Before deferring a cell, require a geometrically compatible, current-epoch parent/coarser cell or cooked visibility result; if none exists, pay current work for that region.

Use fixed group membership and rotation, not Lumen's characteristic-ranked irradiance-probe queue. Preserve complete gather dependency drainage and the accepted gather capacity; bound admissions/ray batches with explicit pending states, rather than silently truncating the gather queue or treating missing cells as open sky. Fallback must use current material/cell programs, not cached final screen colour. A parent covering a wall's other side is invalid even if its normal matches.

**E saving:** from a newly measured refill portion `G`, with schedulable fraction `f` and **E N = 4** groups, peak reduction is `G × f × (1 − 1/N) − O`. For illustration **E G = 2 ms RT / 6 ms software**, `f = 0.5`, overhead `O = 0.1–0.3 ms`, saving is **E 0.45–0.65 ms RT / 1.95–2.15 ms software**. Those G values are assumptions, not quarry measurements. Total work is mostly deferred, not eliminated; later dependency gathers can increase it. If existing current-epoch coverage is absent, **E f = 0** and this candidate has no quality-safe saving. P-SHOW2's **M 4.47 ms** cut GI is a different scene and does not establish quarry G.

**Visible risk:** coarse light leaks, moving blotches, bright new-ground pixels, black patches or a slow brightness ramp. A constant sky fallback can be too bright in crevices and a zero fallback too dark; neither is accepted merely because Lumen sometimes lags. The earlier gather-cap failure is the rejection witness to reproduce, not a reason to call a smaller cap safe.

**Smallest screen, E:** first count parent/epoch/side-compatible fallback coverage from a cut's GI request stream; if sparse, reject before adding scheduling. Then one quarry disocclusion/cut, **12-minute** lease, baseline against grouped admissions with identical accepted gather capacity; record all early final frames and pending queue drainage through **E 64 frames**. **Pass:** bounded GI work with no visible mottle, leaks, brightness ramp or delayed pop during immediate motion; no backlog persisting after return. **Fail:** the old ground/plinth blotches return, pending dependencies starve or a later frame exceeds the old cut peak. Do not try a denoiser to rescue an invalid fallback.

### Fourth: lower-rate current sun visibility only during restart

Evaluate current-world sun visibility on a fixed coarse receiver lattice for the cut, reconstruct it using full-resolution current geometry, and immediately return to full-rate samples. Keep silhouettes, depth/normal/material boundaries and unresolved reconstruction footprints on full-rate current evaluation; keep primary visibility, glossy reflections and material shading at full rate.

This is a screen lattice for querying world geometry, not screen-space shadow transport. Its high risk is missing thin contact/penumbra features between samples; geometry discontinuity masks cannot certify the absence of a shadow boundary on a flat receiver. Thus it ranks below uniform full-coverage sampling and must not merely inherit P-RTS's fringe blind spot.

**E saving:** for a quarter-rate lattice, full-rate rescue fraction `q`, trace cost `T` and reconstruction overhead `O`, save `T × (1 − q) × 3/4 − O`. Illustrative **E q = 0.25**, `O = 0.3–0.8 ms`: using **M old RT cut fresh pass 11.60 ms** as an optimistic scalable proxy yields **E 5.73–6.23 ms**; using **E software scalable march portion 22–28 ms** yields **E 11.58–15.45 ms**. These bounds wrongly credit any fixed work inside T, so count that out before committing. Combined with candidate one, recompute T from the reduced budget; never add both forecasts.

**Visible risk:** light/shadow leaks, detached contacts, blocky edge detail, cut-to-recovery pop and smear if the filter crosses receivers. Use fixed taps and current geometry, a fixed reconstruction radius and spatial reconstruction **before** temporal accumulation; do not grow/shrink a post-temporal filter from history age. A high rescue fraction can erase the entire saving.

**Smallest screen, E:** before building reconstruction, CPU-replay a saved dense cut sun-visibility/receiver buffer onto the proposed lattice and inspect the reconstructed full image against its original. Reject if thin fringe/contact differences are visible even with perfect current dense input. If it survives, one quarry, **10-minute** lease with immediate camera movement, full-rate boundaries and an instrumented rescue count. **Pass:** no contact loss, bright ground dots, block-grid reveal or brightness step, and smaller full-sequence peak. **Fail:** any visible fringe disappears or the rescues erase the gain. A saved dense field cannot validate hidden geometry or actual ray cost; the lease remains necessary.

## Patent checks for the borrowed parts

These are design-around constraints against the flagged claims in [P0](m7-p0-claims.md) and [P-VIA](patent-via-8471853.md), not a legal clearance opinion or a new jurisdiction/status survey. Primary claim text was read, not inferred from abstracts. No patented method is justified by this reading-only study's estimates; the policy's measured-failure fallback has not been reached.

- **Uniform cold-budget reduction / fresh later samples:** no reservoir resampling, no common secondary frustum or ray matching; the known GI RIS combination in [US11315310, claim 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11315310B2/en#C1) is absent; sample-count scheduling alone is not that combination.
- **Retained/prefetched world GI and bounded groups:** avoid characteristic-based ranking and rays from the higher-ranked irradiance location/group required by [US11790594, claims 1 and 10](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11790594B2/en#C1); fixed group membership replaces ranked refresh, and no hashed contribution sum/counter mean is added from [US10614613, claim 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10614613B2/en#C1).
- **Current spatial reconstruction then existing temporal resolve:** keep temporal auxiliary information internal rather than supplying it to subsequent filters as required by [US10600167, claim 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10600167B2/en#C1); fixed taps/radius avoid jittered sparse taps and history-count-driven spatial-filter sizing in [US11113792, claims 1 and 15](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11113792B2/en#C1) and jitter-selected taps in [US12423782, claim 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12423782B2/en#C1).
- **Unreal ideas not borrowed:** no short-history-versus-long-history clamp or lag-selected prior-value queue; those flagged combinations remain outside this plan ([US11216916, claim 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11216916B1/en#C1), [US11847737, claim 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11847737B2/en#C1)); Unreal's implementation is not declared cleared because its behavior is documented.
