What executes each frame

The split sun dispatch is 8x4 adjacent pixels. It visits the frame-sized target but only calls primary_sun for a nonempty primary tile, valid hit and covered reconstructed receiver. Sky/misses write fully visible and clear their held-history entry without sun queries. The receiver contains position, geometric normal, epsilon, thin and slot. fresh_estimate then rejects receivers outside the selected cascade depth interval or light-space box. It is not a penumbra-only list: all eligible sun-covered receivers, including fully lit and fully blocked ones, enter the estimator. P-RTS penumbra listing is disabled by default.

Budget defaults are held 8 after 16 unchanged frames, moving/young 16, restart 32. A receiver inside a swept-caster shadow or a local-light relit zone can receive 32 even on a held or moving frame. A frame-wide reset, including sun turn, can also request 32. Therefore 'held 8 / moving 16' describes base budgets, not the actual query histogram. There is no reuse of last frame's answer without fresh rays in the shipping held-stop path.

On a continuing unturned-sun frame with base budget above 8, an unskinned unmoved receiver outside the swept-caster region can stop after 8 directions if its reprojected 3x3 history is unanimously lit or blocked for at least 4 frames and the 8 new directions agree. Edges, reprojection misses, mixed history, moved/skinned receivers and swept shadows fail this gate. With SUN_ZONE_STOP unset, receivers raised above the base budget by a restart zone cannot stop, even if the restart came only from local RGB lighting. On an ordinary settled base-8 frame the frame-level held gate is disabled (8 is not less than 8); there is nothing to save by an 8-probe stop. It still traces those 8 samples for eligible receivers. Each sample rotates through TR1's sequence and contributes to temporal reconstruction.

Actual hardware query initialization is inside a direction-dependent rate/tmax test, so sample budget and actual ray count are different quantities. A direction that has no valid positive segment performs no query. Rays use the hardware TLAS/BLAS, FORCE_OPAQUE | TERMINATE_ON_FIRST_HIT, and SHADOW_CASTER_MASK. The origin is biased with the receiver epsilon/thin rules. tmax is already clipped to the selected light-space box's forward and side boundaries; it is not infinity. The shipping receiver cascade ends are 4/12/40/1000 m. A cascade receiver-depth limit is not the same thing as a fixed metric shadow-ray tmax.

Counters that exist, and what is still unknown

RENDERLAB_RL_PROBE records reflection slots/hits at frame 30, not sun receivers or rays, and cannot answer this question. RENDERLAB_SUN_RAYS_PROBE replaces the split sun dispatch with an instrumented equivalent at hard-coded renderer frame 32 only. It reports: counters[0] pixels visited; [1] calls entering fresh_estimate (includes coverage skips); [2] actual rayQueryInitialize calls; [3..7] requested 8/16/32/256/other budget populations; [8] held-stop ELIGIBILITY, not successful stops; [9] coverage skips; [10..14] final budgets; [15]/[16]/[17] sampled fully-lit/fully-blocked/mixed receivers; [18] summed requested budget; [19]/[20] eligible32 and its agreeing-eight prefix; and a 0..256 actual-query histogram. The sampled mixed count is not a ground-truth geometric penumbra area: a finite prefix can miss a thin occluder or a penumbra rim.

Candidate levers, ordered by what is already known

1. Fewer settled rays with more temporal accumulation. A 4-ray settled option would halve nominal sample work on unaffected base-8 receivers, not halve sun_shadow: zones, invalid history, coverage/lookup work and query divergence remain. Four is below the current accepted budget/API range and would need a deliberate opt-in implementation. Lower sample count risks grain/shimmer on long soft penumbrae and thin fence/foliage shadows; longer history risks visible lag if it survives a changing light, caster or disocclusion. Cuts, movers, swept shadows, relit receivers and sun changes must keep their fresh budgets and proper invalidation. This is not quarter-refresh; it would still trace fresh samples every frame. Do not estimate a speedup until base-8 query share is measured. An 8-vs-16 moving knob already exists, but cannot lower settled 8 or prove a 4-ray picture.

2. Earlier stops. The existing 8-probe held stop already captures cheap unanimous interiors on moving frames; the cold stop is not a steady-state lever. First measure how much actual work lies in larger-budget receivers that remain unanimous. SUN_ZONE_STOP is an existing opt-in candidate for purely local-relighting restart zones, retaining mover/swept/history guards, but it cannot help ordinary base-8 settled pixels. Smaller probe prefixes or weaker 3x3/streak requirements can falsely classify penumbra rims or thin occluders as unanimous, lock a rim, or lag a moving shadow. A geometrically certified empty/blocked ray bundle would be exact, but needs conservative scene data and its own cost evidence; no generic bounding-box or geometric-normal shortcut is assumed safe here. The rejected P-RTS listing already documents missed thin rims and listing overhead.

3. Cheaper rays. Opaque-only, first-hit termination, caster masks, and light-volume tmax clipping are ALREADY used. There is no unused opacity flag win. A blindly shorter tmax or restoring 40 m receiver coverage removes legitimate distant caster shadows, the defect the 1 km cascade fixed. A tighter tmax is exact only with a conservative proof that no relevant caster lies beyond it; it is not justified by distance alone. Hardware traversal/BLAS layout, instance overlap and ray coherence remain possible cost factors, but the reflection software BVH2 finding does not diagnose any of them. Current evidence does not justify a BLAS rebuild or a shortened shadow reach.

One cheap measurement to rank the remaining choices

One ordinary diagnostic lease: synchronized warm sun-ray census at the same four scene/window states as the completed clean split (Outpost/Showcase x held/moving), on an owned matching binary/project, with SUN_PASS=1, SUN_HISTORY=0, SUN_ZONE_STOP unset. Ask U-RTVIS to supply only a measurement-frame selector for the existing census so it can replace ONE sun dispatch at the selected warm held or moving frame (four short visits, or two selected snapshots per scene if its owner prefers). Do not time the instrumented frame: it compiles a pipeline and performs aggregate atomics. Reuse the existing clean sun times as the cost context and report per-snapshot counts, not an invented multi-frame average.

Read original/final budget histograms, actual-query histogram, sampled mixed population and skips. On a non-cold base-16 moving frame, final-eight minus requested-eight minus skips identifies successful 8-ray held stops under the present probe's default-initialized tr1_rays=8 behavior; an explicit stop counter at the existing break is clearer if its owner adds one. Counter[8] alone must not be labelled stops. Compare N covered, total Q, Q/N, 32-budget share, mixed share and actual-stop share. Then compare Outpost/Showcase Q ratio with the measured 2.8x sun-time ratio. A comparable Q ratio points to receiver/budget demand; similar Q with 2.8x time points to amortized query/traversal/dispatch cost, not ray count. If both differ, report both factors. sun_ms/Q is an AMORTIZED pass cost, not a direct hardware traversal timer; receiver loads/history work and warp divergence remain in it.

This ranks lower settled samples versus more early stops, and closes the already-applied opaque-only idea. Only if counts fail to explain the Outpost/Showcase sun-cost ratio should the next owner-reviewed diagnostic collect shadow-ray segment lengths/committed-hit distances or hardware traversal evidence to evaluate tighter exact bounds.
