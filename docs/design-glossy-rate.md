# Cheaper glossy reflections without a visible regression

Reading-only design study, 2026-11-10. Code baseline: `9e343a0acfef41c8b4b6fbc50bec15f26b316b62` (includes P-DEFECT and P-YARD2). No renderer changes, builds, GPU measurements, or new image inspection were performed. P-SHOW2 is absent at this baseline; P-SHOW, P-DEFECT and P-YARD2 are the latest relevant records.

**Recommendation:** first screen fewer full-resolution glossy strata on the already regularized baseline, keeping the old footprint fixed. If motion detail or the reflected sun fails, keep today's rays and move to separate, reflection-aware reconstruction; do not compensate with longer surface history. This is an experiment order, not a claim that four rays now pass.

## Evidence conventions

- **C:** read from the pinned code; constants and counts are implementation facts, not timings.
- **M:** measured in a named repository report, not reproduced here. Preserve its workload, build and contention caveats.
- **S:** reported by a linked primary source; never normalized to Renderlab hardware.
- **E:** this study's estimate, proposed parameter or acceptance screen, including all arithmetic forecasts below. Formula constants are mathematical definitions. Patent numbers, dates, section numbers and source locators are identifiers.

The commissioning brief's roughly 6 ms RT / 21 ms non-RT reflection cost in a 14.8 ms RT frame is also reported in [P-DEFECT §5](p-defect.md#5-glossy-reflections-method-change-proposal-not-built), but without a new isolated timing table. For forecasts use the more explicit **M** P-SHOW shading ablations: RT `7.76 − 2.24 = 5.52 ms`, non-RT `20.78 − 2.18 = 18.60 ms` ([table](p-show.md#per-pass-breakdown-m), RTX 4070 SUPER, native 1080p, Ara perf profile, per-pass medians). These include inline hit shading and code-generation effects; they are not pure traversal measurements. Different leases and GI states must not be added together.

**M:** [P-YARD2](p-yard.md#full-table-all-presets) records Renderlab's lower full-image reference error, but higher flicker and insufficient speed versus matched Unreal. Its quarry is largely outside the glossy tracing band; it cannot predict a glossy optimization's speedup. Its S4 flat-wall grain also includes GI/shadows, not just reflections ([Flat-surface noise](m7-gaps.md#flat-surface-noise-both-paths)). A reflection-only change cannot claim to fix all of it.

The costly repetition is intersection **and** arbitrary hit shading **and** hit shadow queries per stratum. With one shadowed light, a fully hit glossy pixel can do **C-derived** 16 closest-hit plus 16 shadow walks; additional lights add work. Software's flat instance list, BVH loads and stack multiply that cost. M7-RQ1 bought better motion by spending more rays: **M**, RF8 shading `1.83 → 3.97 ms` RT and `5.57 → 19.19 ms` non-RT, functional/contended 1080p runs ([RQ1 §4.3](m7-rq1.md#43-cost-m)). Increasing rays cannot repair a wrong history trajectory.

### The speck source changed before this study

**M/C:** [P-DEFECT §2](p-defect.md#2-white-pixels-path-regularization-at-reflection-hits) traced the specks to a glossy ray striking a near-mirror surface's very narrow delta-sun highlight. This is high-variance near-specular transport, not evidence that the hit is invalid. Lowering the count makes each rare hit carry more weight. Its proposed probability of roughly one in 200 is diagnostic evidence from that report, not a new count here.

**C:** P-DEFECT is merged: `rl_hit_variance = 8 × spread²` during reflection-hit shading ([reflections.wgsl:172–185](https://github.com/Ara-Engine/renderlab/blob/9e343a0acfef41c8b4b6fbc50bec15f26b316b62/shaders/reflections.wgsl#L172)); engine `brdf` adds it to GGX alpha squared ([scene.wgsl:403–412](https://github.com/Ara-Engine/renderlab/blob/9e343a0acfef41c8b4b6fbc50bec15f26b316b62/shaders/scene.wgsl#L403)). This distributes highlight energy over an angular footprint. It is a biased finite-footprint approximation, not exact caustic transport or a universal firefly remover. Custom programs bypassing `brdf` receive no such protection.

**M:** P-DEFECT turntable held-frame bright-outlier count fell `24 → 2`, versus reference `3`; mean display luma `87.2 → 89.3`, reference `90.8`. Its reported timing was within noise. This is matched-scene evidence, not a fresh Showcase acceptance. **Critical coupling (C):** `cone_alpha = alpha / sqrt(count)`, and `spread` includes twice this value. Reducing count widens texture/environment footprints and hit regularization simultaneously. A faster image can therefore become smoother by accident. Separate sampling rate from footprint when screening.

### Published building blocks, and what each actually estimates

**Ratio estimation:** [Heitz, Hill & McGuire 2018](https://casual-effects.com/research/Heitz2018Shadow/index.html) separates analytically integrated unshadowed illumination from a sampled, illumination-weighted visibility ratio. Frostbite's normalized neighbor resolve similarly estimates a smooth incident-light factor while retaining the receiver's sharp lobe. For a compatible receiver kernel `K`, proposal density `p` and incoming light `L`, an applicable reconstruction form is:

```
A(x)       = integral K(x,w) dw
incident(x)= sum[K(x,w_i) L_i / p_i] / sum[K(x,w_i) / p_i]
reflection = A(x) * incident(x)
```

This is **E**, a candidate adaptation, not Renderlab's existing estimator or a new unbiasedness proof. It is finite-sample biased; denominator support and visibility matter. A neighbor's hit radiance depends on its outgoing direction, especially on mirrors, so it is not generally reusable at another receiver. Weighting cannot fix that. A visibility check may consume the rays being saved. For GGX styles the current DFG/incident split is already a useful demodulation boundary; arbitrary shaders need an opt-in kernel contract or the full-rate path.

**Lower-rate reconstruction:** sparse ray generation must retain current full-resolution receiver identity, lobe and geometry guides. Spatial filtering should precede temporal accumulation, and should filter incident light rather than the complete user-shaded image. Temporal correspondence needs reflected content, not only receiver motion. Near depth discontinuities or new geometry, spend fresh rays rather than trusting absent history. These are proposed Renderlab constraints (**E**), motivated by RQ1/MQ2 and the engine sources above.

**Firefly robustness:** Lumen's firefly-resistant bilateral weights and HDRP's intensity clamp can suppress bright outliers, but cannot promise energy preservation. A robust mean, median or winsorized sum also changes the mean unless there is explicit compensation. [Kaplanyan & Dachsbacher 2013](https://cg.ivd.kit.edu/publications/p2013/PSR_Kaplanyan_2013/PSR_Kaplanyan_2013.pdf) regularizes difficult interactions instead; at a finite radius it trades transport bias for sampleability. Renderlab already takes this route. Do not label its widening unbiased, or call tone-space averaging energy conserving.

**Spatial/temporal sample selection:** [ReSTIR GI](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2021-06_restir-gi-path-resampling-real-time-path-tracing) reuses selected paths to improve scarce-sample efficiency. This is stronger than a fixed weighted gather, but reservoirs, reused path weights and the cache combination enter P4. Motion also creates correlation and validity problems. It is a policy fallback, not the first design.

## 4. Ranked Renderlab candidates

Ranking is by expected value of the **next experiment**, including rejection cost. Savings are not additive: ray reduction removes traversal and hit work that later optimizations would also save.

Shared forecast (**E**): let `C` be P-SHOW's measured reflection-on/off shading difference, `f` the fraction of that cost affected by this candidate, `r` its remaining work ratio, and `O` new overhead. Saving is `C × f × (1 − r) − O`. Unknown ray-weighted roughness distribution, occupancy and shadow-hit rates are folded into explicit assumptions, not called measurements. Mirrors and untraced pixels remain outside ray reduction.

### 1. Re-screen full-resolution four-stratum glossy after regularization

**Idea (two sentences).** Keep every receiver pixel and existing hit-program evaluation, but cap eligible glossy lobes at four strata on the merged P-DEFECT baseline. Freeze the baseline cone, environment footprint and hit-regularization width while changing the sampling rate, so quality cannot pass by secretly adding blur.

**E savings:** where baseline count is 16, `r = 4/16 = 0.25`; at the gold lobe it is `4/12`, and low-roughness lobes gain little. Assume `f = 0.6–0.8`, an unmeasured affected-cost fraction, `O = 0` new passes: RT `5.52 × (0.6–0.8) × 0.75 = 2.48–3.31 ms`; non-RT `18.60 × (0.6–0.8) × 0.75 = 8.37–11.16 ms`. Forecast remaining attributable costs are RT `2.21–3.04 ms`, software `7.44–10.23 ms`, not whole-frame claims. Actual mixed ray counts can be worse. **M precedent only:** P-SHOW's rejected share-scaled variant saved shading `7.8 − 4.7 = 3.1 ms` RT, `25 − 11.5 = 13.5 ms` non-RT; that older variant used a different selection rule and failed the image.

**Specks / motion:** regularization makes the known sun path less extreme, but fixed-cone fewer rays can still miss it. No fix to receiver-history lag; RQ1 already demonstrated four-ray motion smear before P-DEFECT. Increase neither history duration nor footprint to hide it. P-DEFECT's custom-BRDF exception remains.

**Programmability:** no cached shader output; same hit inputs and `surface`/`shade` calls. Same-program content is immediately eligible. Do not certify mixed-program fidelity from today's fallback; retaining this on mixed content requires candidate 2's own-program hit dispatch, even if its reconstruction is disabled.

**Claims:** P4/P5 strongest avoidance: own-pixel ordinary strata, no neighbor candidates or projections. No new filter/history, so P1–P3 unchanged. P6 unchanged.

**Cheap rejection:** CPU-count `sum(min(K,4))/sum(K)` from existing lobe/roughness captures, weighted separately by hit/shadow frequency if recorded. Existing colour PNGs cannot recover that count. Replay recorded per-ray radiance only if available (a subset of old strata is an opportunity/noise screen, not an exact replay of the new stratum pattern); arbitrary WGSL cannot be faithfully replayed from final RGB. If unavailable, first future GPU screen is the existing matched turntable plus spinning gold/crate, cut and short motion, both paths, one process with paired variants; reject at the first visible speck, lost glow or motion-detail regression. No architecture build is needed to reject this count experiment. Keep today's count on unsupported styles.

### 2. Separate reflection signal; lower-rate world rays and fixed-tap incident reconstruction

**Idea (two sentences).** Trace and record fewer rays separately from receiver shading, shade hits in lists keyed by the hit's own program, and reconstruct only the declared incident-light lobe using current-frame fixed taps before one reflection-aware temporal stage. Begin at full receiver rate with a reduced stratum count, then screen a fixed sparse lattice on broad smooth-in-screen glossy regions while preserving full-rate mirrors, boundaries and incompatible programs.

**E savings:** a quarter of eligible receiver pixels with four strata instead of 16 gives `r = 1/16`; that is an aggressive upper experiment, not the initial default. Assume `f = 0.8` and **E** overhead `O = 0.5–1.0 ms` RT / `0.7–1.5 ms` software for queues, program dispatch and reconstruction: RT `5.52 × 0.8 × 15/16 − O = 3.14–3.64 ms`; software `18.60 × 0.8 × 15/16 − O = 12.45–13.25 ms`. Initial full-rate/four-strata stage with the same assumptions instead saves RT `2.31–2.81 ms`, software `9.66–10.46 ms`. These overheads have no Renderlab measurement; visibility rechecks or full-rate rescue work can erase the savings. Count rescued pixels before believing the sparse forecast.

**Data and ownership (E):** hit record retains placement, primitive, barycentrics, ray origin/direction, cone, receiver ID, stratum, proposal PDF and hit distance/miss flag. Per-program lists call the actual hit program and restore nothing from the receiver's shader state; software and hardware produce the same semantic record. Receivers consume filtered incident RGB through `specular_incident`, then their own program composites it. Custom receiver kernels opt in to normalized reconstruction; unsupported/non-GGX styles and strongly view-dependent hit programs keep independent own-pixel shading. This is a real dispatch change, not the current mixed-program fallback renamed.

**Reconstruction / motion (E):** fixed nonseparable current-frame taps, receiver plane/normal/lobe/material compatibility and no neighbor sample-survivor selection. If reuse crosses a visibility or hit-program boundary, reject it or trace fresh; do not interpolate a bright foreground hit into a dark receiver. Use planar mirrored-camera projection only to locate **history** for known planes, not to render common secondary projections; a single mean hit distance is insufficient on a curved reflector or a lobe spanning several depths. Start curved/high-depth-spread pixels with geometry-capped short history and fresh rays. Carry hit object's previous transform for moving reflected content; reject topology, program, lighting-event and disocclusion changes. Temporal auxiliary outputs stay private under P1; final TR1 sees RGB without reflection confidence/age. Reflection temporal history followed by TR1 colour accumulation can still double-lag, so screen this explicitly and reduce the reflection window rather than exporting its age to TR1.

**Specks:** keep P-DEFECT's fixed baseline width. A fixed current-frame lobe gather can distribute a legitimate bright sample, but does not guarantee preserving per-receiver energy or correct visibility. Never suppress an outlier solely because it is bright; validate mean radiance and highlight extent against reference. Motion should improve only where history correspondence is valid; sparse cuts can reveal checker patterns and need rescue rays. Do not ship an excess-energy long history: deferred energy becomes a trail when the light/object moves.

**Claims:** P1 mandates spatial-before-temporal and auxiliary isolation. P2 fixes screen taps and radius; traced-direction rotation may continue. P3 forbids variance-derived confidence and fast history. P4 uses all compatible taps without RIS. P5 lacks common projection and reflector-triangle matching, but neighbor reassignment is a thinner boundary requiring focused claim review before implementation. Program sorting preserves ray origins and global traversal. No new world cache (P6).

**Cheap rejection:** on saved raw full-resolution incident/hit/receiver buffers, CPU-mask a fixed lattice and run the normalized gather; compare turntable glow, RF thin rails, grazing puddle and gold fine/mid-band detail. On available motion buffers compare planar virtual correspondence against surface correspondence and count curved/depth-boundary rescue demand. Reject if the compatible/rescue counts cannot meet `C × f × (1−r) > O`, or if one cut crop visibly loses reflected objects. Final PNGs cannot supply these records; request one small future buffer capture if absent, not a full GPU campaign. Do not build the sparse pipeline before this screen.

### 3. Cheaper exact software traversal and independent hit dispatch

**Idea (two sentences).** Keep today's rays while replacing the flat instance scan / binary traversal with an instance hierarchy and a compressed wide tree that preserves drawn-triangle identity. Separate hit-program dispatch can also remove hit-shading register pressure from traversal, with both backends sharing records and program lists.

**E savings:** software-only traversal speedup `s = 1.5–2` and traversal fraction `f = 0.5–0.7` give `18.60 × f × (1−1/s) = 3.10–6.51 ms`, before new dispatch overhead. With **E** `O = 0.3–0.7 ms`, net `2.40–6.21 ms`. These fractions are not Showcase profiles; [non-RT design](design-nonrt-reflections.md) measures a different game-base traversal fraction of **M** 31% and must not be substituted. ### 4. Rough-band visibility ratio / existing environment control variate

**Idea (two sentences).** On explicitly compatible broad lobes, keep an analytically prefiltered environment and estimate only its visibility correction, adding independently shaded local-hit radiance. Use a few own-pixel world rays and deterministic integration, with no roughness-threshold expansion until local reflected detail survives.

**E savings:** assume only `f = 0.25` of cost is eligible and replace 16 rays by two (`r = 1/8`), with **E** `O = 0.2 ms` RT / `0.4 ms` software: `5.52 × 0.25 × 7/8 − 0.2 = 1.01 ms` RT, `18.60 × 0.25 × 7/8 − 0.4 = 3.67 ms` software. **Specks / flicker:** improves sampling of smooth environment energy, not mirror-sun highlights on local geometry; those retain own-program regularization. Fewer geometry samples can flicker at occlusion edges; a sky-weighted visibility ratio is not valid for arbitrary incoming local radiance. A safer additive control variate estimates `integral K E + mean[K(L−E)/p]` with fresh hit shading, but signed residuals can remain noisy. This alternative is an **E** estimator proposal, not measured engine behavior. Do not substitute mean unweighted visibility or diffuse irradiance for glossy radiance.

**Programmability / claims:** actual local hits still execute their program, never use cached final output; receiver opts in to the lobe/analytic control contract. P4 no RIS, P5 own-pixel world rays, P6 no new cache averages or ranked update scheduling; any spatial/temporal filter must obey P1–P3. A roughness cutoff alone proves neither image quality nor avoidance.

**Cheap rejection:** count ray-weighted eligible rough-band pixels from existing captures, and CPU-evaluate environment-only, occluded-sky and local-bright-object cases with the estimator.
