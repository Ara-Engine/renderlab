# Motion stability without hiding changes in history

Reading-only study, 2026-11-10, baseline `9e343a0acfef41c8b4b6fbc50bec15f26b316b62`. No renderer edits, builds, GPU work, new captures or replay measurements. `docs/p-still.md` is absent at this baseline. The earlier glossy study is [PR #4](https://github.com/Ara-Engine/renderlab/pull/4); this note stands independently and does not assume its proposals have shipped.

**Recommendation:** first screen extra fresh shadow samples for geometrically young/revealed receivers, keeping TR1's existing lag limits. Before implementing the classifier, measure how much residual error is actually in that population; switch to GI source reconstruction if it is not large enough. Longer history, more permissive clamping and unconditional firefly suppression are not substitutes for that attribution.

## Evidence and the actual target

- **M:** measured in the named repo report, not reproduced here. Retain its scene, build, exposure and contention qualifications.
- **S:** reported by a linked primary publication or engine document, not a Renderlab prediction.
- **E:** this study's estimate, inference or proposed experiment, including every cost forecast, scenario and proposed parameter below. Formulas define their variables mathematically.
- **C (reported by source):** constants and behavior from the pinned repository implementation. These are source-reported implementation facts, not GPU measurements. Patent numbers, source dates and line/section identifiers are locators.

**M:** [P-YARD2](p-yard.md#full-table-all-presets) measured native 1080p, RTX 4070 SUPER, Renderlab `71ea355`, Unreal 5.7.4 Lumen + TSR, matched quarry cameras and retained path-traced references. Its moving-frame residual flicker is **4.39 RT / 4.44 non-RT**, versus **3.68 HW Epic / 3.69 SW Epic**, in display codes. Renderlab's lower static/reference error does not negate this visible motion loss. The frames compare complete pipelines; they do not isolate TSR from Lumen or TR1 from GI.

The score is the RGB RMS of `(image_t − image_previous) − (reference_t − reference_previous)` on consecutive moving frames, not raw temporal variance. **C:** the actual mask intersects both references' all-covered masks and requires the same reference placement label at the same screen pixel; it is not motion-compensated surface tracking. Initial reveals that change that label are excluded, while some young-history and near-edge pixels remain. The summary pools squared residual by pixel count before taking its square root ([score.py:139–173](https://github.com/Ara-Engine/renderlab/blob/9e343a0acfef41c8b4b6fbc50bec15f26b316b62/tools/p-yard/score.py#L139)). A truly changing light or moving shadow must change. A frozen frame can look steady and still fail this residual; low-pass filtering can also reduce noise while increasing lag. Use exactly the original scorer and masks for the headline, and separate surface-aligned measurements for diagnosis.

**E arithmetic:** reaching 3.68 from 4.39 requires about **29.7% less squared residual**, `1 − (3.68/4.39)^2`. Non-RT to 3.69 needs about **30.9%**, `1 − (3.69/4.44)^2`. These are arithmetic targets, not measured removable-noise fractions. The fraction attributable to each subsystem, and their cross-covariances, are unknown.

**M:** P-YARD's [S4 inspection](p-yard.md#crop-sheets-inspected-by-eye) reports heavy flat-wall/door grain on both paths, visible after the door stops, plus stray RT bright pixels on a moving door edge and newly revealed ground. It records no reference for that S4 view and no visible door trail on either engine. Thus the quarry number, S4 grain and S4 bright pixels are separate observations. Reading does **not** prove that those bright pixels are the glossy mirror-sun defect fixed by P-DEFECT.

TR1 therefore both removes variation and exposes it deliberately when retaining history would blur or trail. Matching the same surface does not make its moving shadow, indirect illumination, animated shader or reflection unchanged. Final-colour accumulation cannot distinguish those components after a user program combines them.

**M historical controls:** [TR1](m7-tr1.md#53-what-tr1-wins-and-loses) beat TSR's held-frame step, **0.244 vs 0.432 codes**, while losing moving edges. [TR1b](m7-tr1b.md#22-the-hold-step-regression-and-its-fix) reduced a hold-step regression by keeping stable two-surface pairs; the problematic energy was concentrated at multi-surface corners. **M counterexample to longer history:** [TR2 §2.2](m7-tr2.md#22-rejected-catmull-rom-next-to-edges-m) increased near-edge history to 16, then 32 samples. Hold step improved **0.222 → 0.207 → 0.182**, but moving isolated/narrow populations worsened and revealed-age error did not improve. It was rejected. TR2's shipped young-history interpolation affects upscaling presets; P-YARD is native, so it is not already a native flicker fix.

### 1.2 Which events reset or cap history now

**C**, [reconstruction_key:826–895](https://github.com/Ara-Engine/renderlab/blob/9e343a0acfef41c8b4b6fbc50bec15f26b316b62/src/temporal_reconstruction.rs#L826), [continuation/reset:1113–1167](https://github.com/Ara-Engine/renderlab/blob/9e343a0acfef41c8b4b6fbc50bec15f26b316b62/src/temporal_reconstruction.rs#L1113):

- Changed program/prelude, geometry identity, reconstruction bindings, material/texture/environment state, placements/topology, shadow/visibility settings or local-light shape/position/direction/range changes invalidate the key. Missing consecutive reconstruction state, explicit cut or sufficiently large sun turn also restart. Exposure rescales accepted history rather than blindly resetting it.
- Rigid transforms, normal transforms and skin poses no longer unconditionally invalidate the global key. MQ1/MQ2 and later shared motion support already fixed those historical resets; R1n's transform-upload misclassification was fixed too. Do not propose these again as new work.
- Local candela/colour bits were removed from the key, but **default `light_budget = 0` still explicitly restarts the whole image when they change**. More changed local lights than the relit block can hold also restart. The optional positive budget caps near-light history and uses the motion box; it is not the accepted default.
- Changed skinned poses reject static receivers inside the swept shadow influence. With one shadowed sun, current code tests the disk-expanded swept bounds along the light ray; other configurations retain broader fallback bounds. This is more selective than P-FLAT's original stretched box. Motion of a reflected target or a shadow on a static receiver still needs illumination validation beyond receiver identity.

**M:** [P-FLAT-TR1](p-flat-tr1.md#31-flicker-history-must-follow-the-fire-flicker_split2py-scriptsp-flat-motionpy) kept default global light resets because clamp-only history lagged a real flickering fire: RT wall response slope **0.985 → 0.861**, with unity meaning faithful response to the no-history control. Even the proposed cap/restart variant left a small measured lag. The stale intensity can lie inside the current spatial gradient's min/max box, particularly near a fire's turning point. An adjacent-frame light delta does not bound all older light states in history.

This is an important game-scene failure, but P-YARD's quarry lighting is fixed. **E:** disabling global local-light resets alone should have essentially no effect on its headline flicker, and cannot be advertised as the answer to 4.39.

## 2. How other systems manage the same trade

No published method here guarantees absence of ghosting in arbitrary motion. The transferable idea is to accumulate only what is valid, improve fresh estimates when it is not, and represent motion/material exceptions explicitly. Source-reported timings below are for complete methods on their own workloads, never incremental Renderlab forecasts.

## 4. Ranked experiments and their expected effect

Ranked by the next useful experiment, including rejection effort. Candidate 1 is a narrow, falsifiable first screen, not an assertion that shadows dominate. No experiment changes the allowed blur/ghost envelope to pass.

### Reading a score forecast correctly

For **E sensitivity arithmetic only**, assume a fraction `f` of squared residual belongs to the affected source/population and the candidate multiplies that component's amplitude by `a`, leaving other components uncorrelated and unchanged. Then `F_new = F_old × sqrt(1 − f × (1−a²))`. Actual source residuals can correlate; compute the full residual from replay when possible. Population masks partition squared error exactly; ablating a lighting component does not, because cross terms remain. Forecasts below are conditional planning scenarios, not measured predictions or additive gains. Null gain remains possible for every unbuilt candidate.

### 1. Fresh estimates for geometrically young receivers

**Idea (E):** retain the current TR1 windows and rejection, but spend a larger current-frame sun visibility budget on newly revealed receivers and motion-edge regions identified from current/previous **raw geometry**. Begin with the existing sample sequence and a larger prefix so that the sampling-rate change is isolated; do not simultaneously change clamping, jitter or history length.

Current frame-level active budgets do not distinguish a well-tracked interior from a freshly revealed penumbra. A geometry classifier can reconstruct prior visibility using the same underlying motion records as TR1 without reading TR1's filtered age/coverage/weights. Keep it upstream of shading and independent. If a local-light or GI component, rather than sun visibility, dominates a crop, extra sun rays are the wrong work. Invalid software geometry/proxy hits are correctness work, not a sample-count problem.

**E expected score:** doubling an independent Monte Carlo sample count suggests `a² ≈ 1/2` for the affected random component, not for bias/coverage/GI. At assumed `f = 0.25–0.60`, RT **4.39 → 4.11–3.67**, software **4.44 → 4.15–3.71**. To reach RT's HW-Epic target by this mechanism alone needs about **59.5%** of squared residual to be removable at that rate. These large fractions are unmeasured; if the attributable fraction is small, expect only a small whole-frame gain. Low-discrepancy correlations can invalidate square-root scaling.

**E cost:** illustrative active prefix **32 → 64** rays for fraction `p = 0.02–0.10` of pixels. [P-FLAT-TR1 §4](p-flat-tr1.md#4-cost) derives an old, unmeasured RT slope of about **0.045 ms per additional full-screen ray** from RTDEF2 data; using that **estimate**, extra ray cost is `32 × p × 0.045 = 0.029–0.144 ms`. Add **E 0.03–0.08 ms** classification: roughly **+0.06–0.23 ms RT**. For software, no corresponding current slope exists; assuming **E 2–5×** this ray increment gives roughly **+0.09–0.80 ms** including classification. This broad range is a sizing guess, not a measured software/hardware ratio. Preserve existing proven-clear/blocked shortcuts and count actual extra walks. Do not double rays globally.

**Ghost/smear risk:** low added temporal risk because no older colour is retained; a wrong classifier wastes rays and may miss problem pixels. Preserve the current penumbra shape, receiver epsilon and sample footprint. Cost can rise substantially on cuts; cap the classified work conservatively and keep a clear quality fallback.

**Claims:** P-A geometric data only; P-C W5 preserved; no temporal image-difference confidence (P-D), new filters (P-E), or cache/RIS changes (P-F). Ray-count selection comes from geometry, not the same samples later averaged.

**Reject before building:** partition existing P-YARD residual frames into interior, edge and young-history masks using raw visibility/motion if retained, intersecting each with the scorer's actual same-placement mask; track first-reveal quality separately because those pixels may be excluded from the headline; inspect pre-TR1 visibility and raw radiance at S4's bright pixels. Replay saved visibility directions to compare larger prefixes if available. If young pixels contribute little inside that headline mask, this can still improve S4 reveal quality but cannot explain the quarry score. If the maximum removable energy is below the required target, record the bounded partial gain and switch to candidate 2 rather than promising parity. If raw records are absent, one small future diagnostic capture is needed; final PNGs cannot recover per-ray visibility. No such replay was run here.

### 2. Repair GI input discontinuity and outliers before final-colour history

**Idea (E):** use a current-frame cell-space replay to distinguish stale/partial cache publication, neighbor/level transitions and genuine rare bright transport, then correct the responsible source operation. Start with deterministic, geometry-compatible interpolation of valid raw incident cells at boundaries; retain G3's existing filter as the control, not as a feature to rediscover.

A cache sample must be valid for the receiver's side/plane and lighting state. Publishing a coherent fixed-direction result can prevent a partial fill flash, but retaining an old complete result after a lighting change would create GI lag; use current conservative fallback/recomputation rather than hide it in a second temporal cache. Deterministic interpolation of valid cells can still blur contacts or leak across a thin wall, so require current geometry compatibility and preserve the unfiltered comparison. Increase directions locally only after a replay proves sampling variance, not invalid cache identity, is the cause. Do not rank cache locations by measured colour/age importance (P-F).

**E expected score:** if GI/discontinuity noise is `f = 0.30–0.60` and its amplitude can be halved, RT **4.39 → 3.86–3.26**, software **4.44 → 3.91–3.29**. This is an optimistic source-isolation scenario, not a measured GI fraction. Replacing missing bounce by darkness can lower flicker while failing reference quality; that result is rejected.

**E cost:** a fixed neighboring-cell gather and validity tests budget **+0.10–0.35 ms RT / +0.15–0.50 ms software** at native 1080p, assuming a memory-local query and no new visibility rays. These are unmeasured engineering budgets, not source timings. Extra cell visibility rays or synchronous drain can exceed them. Removing the held-skin software relight scaffolding might save time, but only after fresh Showcase quality evidence; [P-SHOW](p-show.md) and P-DEFECT's earlier timings are not a current guaranteed saving.

**Ghost/smear risk:** no added temporal window, but current-space interpolation can soften indirect contacts and mix visibility. Reject any delayed lighting response, light leak or loss of crevice contrast. Do not use a slowly blended cache to obtain stability.

**Claims:** P-F fixed values/schedules, no counted hashed mean or ranked updates; P-E fixed nonseparable neighborhood, no jittered taps; P-C filter precedes TR1 and consumes no TR1 auxiliaries; P-D no lighting-gradient confidence. Custom program output is still evaluated: reconstruct incident inputs, not cached final receiver RGB.

**Reject before building:** replay raw cell keys, query points, validity, incident coefficients and final receiver queries for a short S4/quarry segment; mark residuals coincident with level switches, late gathers or cell publication. Count compatible neighbors before proposing more interpolation. Compare the existing G3 source and filtered values; reject a new kernel if the needed extra support crosses walls or attenuates contact detail. If neither cell transitions nor GI outliers explain the crop, switch to geometry/history investigation instead of broadening the filter.

### 3. Improve moving-edge resampling and validate hidden layers

**Idea (E):** audit the second history layer's geometric acceptance, then screen geometry-clipped reconstruction of the visible portion of a footprint. Keep the same maximum windows; aim to avoid repeated bilinear blur and invalid bright-layer transfers rather than extend history near an edge.

The second slot currently lacks a stored point/plane anchor. Adding a compact geometric anchor or reconstructible prior hit identity could reject stale colour from a different fold of the same placement. More rejection alone can increase flicker, so combine acceptance changes only with a demonstrated better fresh footprint estimate, in separate replay variants. Multi-surface corners need a principled fallback to current samples, not resurrection of an unobserved bright layer. Correct rigid/skinned motion already exists and remains the baseline.

**E expected score:** for `f = 0.10–0.30` in affected edge/coverage residuals and `a = 0.5`, RT **4.39 → 4.22–3.86**, software **4.44 → 4.27–3.91**. If S4's bright pixels are entirely raw shading outliers, this candidate may remove none of them. Stricter acceptance without better current sampling may make the score worse.

**E cost:** **+0.05–0.25 ms RT / +0.05–0.30 ms software** for extra history-anchor traffic and geometry tests; both are guesses. Memory grows if another anchor is stored. Do not silently allocate a whole extra colour history. The historical TR1b resolve was **M 0.404–0.405 ms median RT** in paired screens; that is context, not today's measured budget or a proof these additions fit.

**Ghost/smear risk:** rejecting a wrong layer should reduce ghosts, but false geometric matches or a wide plane tolerance can retain them. Geometry-clipped footprint transport can lose curved/thin geometry; do not substitute a wider spatial tent. Preserve coverage motion separately for foreground/background objects.

**Claims:** P-A geometric correspondence; P-B no variance/clipped-statistical box; P-C retain one value per surface and private auxiliary state, no lag-selected history lists. Spatial current-sample kernel, if any, must obey P-E. P0 notes ambiguity around directional kernels: avoid claiming any “non-variance” resampler is automatically clear.

**Reject before building:** CPU-replay the existing straight-edge coverage model, thin rails, coplanar folds, two objects with different motion and a three-surface bright corner using recorded raw geometry or analytic fixtures. Compare correct coverage, stale-layer acceptance, moving edge spread and residual error. Re-run TR2's rejected longer-window variant only as a negative control in offline replay; do not rebuild it as a proposed fix.

### 4. Better sample spectra with unchanged counts

**Idea (E):** screen spatially and temporally stratified ray directions in place of independently hashed screen-pixel shifts, keeping ray count, estimator, footprints and TR1 fixed. Include the existing paired primary jitter as a control; optimize ray sequences separately from camera jitter so their effects remain attributable.

**E expected score:** modest uncertain gain. If `f = 0.30` is affected and amplitude falls to `a = 0.8`, RT **4.39 → 4.15**, software **4.44 → 4.19**; zero gain under moving-surface temporal decorrelation is plausible. No published fixed-pixel STBN spectrum justifies forecasting full target parity on this camera path. Surface-keyed sequences can remain correlated with geometry and create structured noise, so compare static bias and motion together.

**E cost:** roughly **0–0.08 ms** on each path for a small mask lookup or sequence permutation at native 1080p, excluding offline mask generation and memory residency; estimate only. Trace work may change even at equal ray count because directions hit different geometry. Retain unbiased proposal weighting and full-support sampling; never bias rays away from expensive or bright directions.

**Ghost/smear risk:** no history change, hence no designed new lag; visible structured patterns or periodic shimmer remain possible. Freezing sample directions can convert flicker into permanent grain, which is not success.

**Claims:** P-E distinguishes ray directions from spatial-filter tap locations. P-B/P-C/P-D/P-F combinations remain absent. Importing an SDK mask requires license/claim checking; this proposal is a sampling experiment, not wholesale denoiser adoption.

**Reject before building:** CPU-integrate analytic moving half-plane/sun-disk visibility and glossy highlight functions using both sequences at the existing counts. Compare spatial error, frame differences, low-frequency power and mean bias after the exact TR1 blend weights. Replay a moving pixel trajectory, not only a stationary screen pixel. If it improves only held pixels, it does not answer P-YARD motion.

### 5. Localize engine-event resets with conservative influence

**Idea (E):** on local-light intensity changes, immediately restart all potentially affected receivers while allowing unrelated receivers to retain history. Use submitted light/geometry/material events and an optional program semantic declaration; include reflected and indirect influence, with a conservative global fallback when it cannot be bounded.

A direct light's range alone does not bound its bounce or the screen area of its reflection. A world-local invalidation limited to that range could leave stale lighting elsewhere. Do not ship P-FLAT's positive light budget unchanged: its measured lag is already the reason it is opt-in. Do not compare only adjacent light values; if a cap is explored, it must bound all contributing light states or reset, and it still needs claim review if the bound comes from rendered lighting.

**E expected score:** approximately **no change to the fixed-light quarry headline**. On the flickering-light courtyard, use only the measured opportunity in [P-FLAT-TR1](p-flat-tr1.md#32-crates-and-other-flat-surfaces-scriptsp-flat-scorepy-frames-8-47): **M** opt-in RT crate speckle **0.079 → 0.059**, software **0.278 → 0.206**, but with measured response lag. These are an upper lead for a better reset scheme, not an acceptable result or a forecast in P-YARD units. Unknown indirect influence may force global reset and give no gain.

**E cost:** **+0.02–0.10 ms** per path for a small event buffer and receiver tests, excluding extra source sampling; CPU scene traversal and large-event cases are unpriced. Continuing unaffected pixels may save shadow rays if frame-wide reset budgets can also become selective, but no saving is claimed before counts.

**Ghost/smear risk:** high if influence bounds omit GI, reflected targets or custom shaders. Correctly conservative resets add no temporal lag; expose source noise rather than average a stale light. Optional program-authored “history unsafe” semantics should only reduce reuse, never certify arbitrary colour as unchanged.

**Claims:** P-A engine events, P-D no per-pixel lighting-gradient confidence, P-C no fast history/list selection. Preserve P-B current extrema only. A stored min/max of engine light parameters is different from a history of pixel colours, but do not claim a rendered-light-change confidence is clear merely because it is called a light budget.

**Reject before building:** evaluate event bounds over saved world hit positions, then include explicit light-bounce and reflected-light witnesses outside direct range. Reject the local rule if those witnesses retain stale colour. Regress mean linear-light response against the no-history control as P-FLAT did; any lower response slope or longer step settling disqualifies the variant despite reduced grain.

### 6. Reflection-aware history and declared reactive materials

**Idea (E):** give specular incident light its own correct correspondence where it can be established, rather than treating the receiver's motion as reflected motion. Programs can declare animated or untrackable shading to shorten reuse; other hit lighting remains programmable, including the unresolved mixed-program hit-dispatch requirement.

**E expected score:** little or no predicted gain on the largely untraced rough quarry. On reflective fixtures, **M MQ2** improved chrome flicker **8.11 → 6.21** against a supersampled control by capping lag; that does not predict the P-YARD score or certify new reflected-depth history. The glossy study's geometry/incident reconstruction remains the next reference for a method experiment; no additional numeric reflection gain is claimed here.

**E cost:** **+0.2–0.8 ms RT / +0.3–1.0 ms software** for reflection records/history and correct program dispatch/reconstruction, before any savings from fewer rays; coarse unmeasured budgets. A material semantic flag alone is estimated below **0.05 ms** per path when piggybacked on current outputs, but a new target/pass may cost more.

**Ghost/smear risk:** a single hit distance is not enough for a curved reflector or broad lobe spanning multiple depths. Moving reflected objects need their previous transforms; unknown correspondence must reject and obtain fresh samples. Reactive masks can expose noise and cannot replace missing motion. S4's door-edge specks must be attributed before treating them as this problem.

**Claims:** P-A authored semantics, P-C private single-history state and no downstream auxiliary flow, P-D no image-gradient confidence, P-E fixed spatial reconstruction taps, P-F no RIS/cache resampling. Common-projection/matching reflection patents require the glossy study's separate P0 §3.E constraints. Do not adopt TSR-style history resurrection or a two-speed denoiser to rescue uncertain reflection motion.

**Reject before building:** replay planar mirror motion and a curved glossy reflector against known moving target geometry; measure hit correspondence, rejection fraction and ghost extent. Reject if the only passing result requires longer surface history or loses the moving highlight. Final RGB captures alone do not contain the required reflection-hit motion.

## 5. Recommendation and decision gate

First perform a short causal ledger on the existing P-YARD witness frames: pre/post TR1 radiance, raw geometry, sun visibility, GI query/validity, and both layer/coverage values at the observed outliers. Analysis may use TR1 auxiliaries offline; production stages may not consume them as new filter controls. This is measurement tooling, not permission to change any rendering defaults in this PR. Reuse retained records if present; otherwise one cropped future capture is needed.

**First implementation candidate:** selectively improve current-frame sun estimates on young receivers (candidate 1), provided the ledger confirms a meaningful shadow-noise contribution. It keeps the existing no-smear history envelope and has a simple CPU rejection screen. Its aim is better current input, not more history.

**Switch to candidate 2** if raw GI/cell transitions dominate flat walls/outliers, or the shadow-noise energy bound cannot justify the added rays. The formula's roughly 59.5% requirement is a test of a claim to close the whole RT gap by doubling samples, not a requirement to discard smaller useful wins. Retain every faster or acceptably cheap visibly better step; record the remaining score honestly. If raw input is stable but the output creates bright edges, switch directly to candidate 3. Sampling-spectrum and local-reset work remain small independent screens, not reasons to delay an identified source fix.

Do not raise global history windows, enable the existing positive light budget, add variance clipping, resurrect older frames or clamp bright values as the first response. Repo experiments already show that a lower hold-step/grain score can purchase lag, smear or lost energy. Patented fallbacks remain named/off until the required failed-alternative evidence exists.

### A small screen that can actually reject ghosting

**E proposed future verification**, on a GPU host only:

- Use the frozen P-YARD camera/reference identities and unchanged exposure/scorer on both paths. Pair variants in one warm process, but reset GI/atmosphere/history explicitly for replay equivalence; [TR2](m7-tr2.md#22-rejected-catmull-rom-next-to-edges-m) documents cache state contaminating in-process variant comparisons. Preserve the current source base and content manifest.
- Add S4's moving door/revealed ground crop, TR1b's thin/three-surface edges, a spinning glossy target and P-FLAT's real flickering fire. S4 lacks a matching path-traced reference: its no-history/current-frame and no-trail controls are necessary, and no reference-quality claim is available there from the existing report.
- Score the original residual flicker, reference detail/error, moving edge spread and ghost extent together. Inspect short forward/backward motion strips at normal display scale. A held still or lower average RMS alone cannot pass.
- For light changes use both step and oscillating events, response slope and settling delay, including indirect/reflected witnesses. Lower grain that follows the light less faithfully is rejected. Freeze-sequence variants must also pass static bias/grain so flicker is not merely replaced by fixed error.
- Start with one paired short screen; broaden only retained changes. Log per-population ray counts, history rejection, GI generations and GPU pass/span costs. Each retained variant needs an independent RT/non-RT verdict, with no global superiority claim from one crop.

## 6. What reading cannot establish

The **4.39/4.44 source-energy split is unknown**; none of the score scenarios is a measurement. Current high-budget shadows may already make candidate 1 a small opportunity. S4's bright edge/ground pixels have no recorded pre-TR1 causal trace, and are not proven reflection fireflies. Flat-wall grain can be GI, visibility, history or a combination; the older courtyard lamp/reset diagnosis is not a completed S4 diagnosis.

Windows raw evidence was not accessed here, and retention rules deleted many raw captures. No CPU replay or current-base GPU cost was measured. The historical reports use different bases, scenes, budgets and controls; speckle, per-pixel maxima, response slope and P-YARD RMS flicker are not interchangeable units. P-STILL is not present at the pinned base.

Neither current-program declarations nor receiver motion prove arbitrary shading is temporally stable. Correct reflection hit-program dispatch, unmodeled vertex animation, transparent layers, recursive light transport and changing indirect influence remain constraints. Estimated millisecond budgets need actual profiling; no universal no-ghosting guarantee or complete patent clearance follows from this note.
