## 2. What G2 builds

### 2.1 Transport

A cell's value was its occluded sky alone (G1). G2 adds the light its blocked directions see, in a second L1 SH in the hot record (words 16-22: an f32 scale and twelve halves), so shading reads both in the same record load.

1. **Gather** (`gi_gather`, eight lanes a cell, eight cells a workgroup): each blocked fixed direction of a queued cell traces one closest-hit ray. The hit decodes to the receiver a pixel there would have: placement, triangle, barycentrics and the interpolated vertex normal turned to the side the ray sees (`gi_hit_receiver`). Its cell at its own level is read, with up to `GI_PARENT_ROUNDS` coarser levels after it, and that cell's last outgoing radiance is weighted by 2π/K into the L1 SH. A fixed tree sums the lanes, and the sum **replaces** the cell's bounce value. There's no blend, counter or average (W1 form 1).
2. **Missing hit cells** are requested at the gathering cell's priority band. Pixel-made cells request at [2^30, 2^31), and cells their gathers made request at [1, 2^30). Cells made for a secondary gather are tertiary and request nothing. Priority orders inserts within a frame's fixed claim; it never decides which rays are cast (W2).
3. **Light visibility** (`gi_light`): one ray per light from each queued cell's query point (hardware rays on RT, Virtual/software rays on non-RT), one bit per light in the cold record. Shadow internals are called, not changed.
4. **Cell shading** (`gi_shade_cells`, [gi_shade_entry.wgsl](../shaders/gi_shade_entry.wgsl)): one thread per queued cell builds the `SurfaceInput` at the cell's fixed query point and runs the frame program's own `surface`. It sums:
   - `emission`;
   - `scatter` times each visible light;
   - the program's hemispherical response (scatter over 16 fixed directions, exact for Lambert) times the cell's own incident sky plus bounce over π.

   The result is the cell's view-independent outgoing radiance (`gi_outgoing`, 16 B a slot), which the next frame's gathers read.
5. **Scheduling, without ranking (W2).**
   - Each event (a new or re-traced cell, or a lighting, environment, program or caster step) gives every cell it reaches a countdown of G = 3 gathers (`IndirectSettings::gathers`), plus one retry when a gather met hit cells without a value. G gathers carry G + 1 bounces.
   - Lighting, environment and caster steps relight fixed slot groups, `slot % 8 == phase`, while they keep changing, then catch up over eight frames. A single step relights everything at once.
   - The gather queues are double-buffered, so a cell reads only the previous frame's outgoing radiance and results don't depend on thread order.
   - With bounce, a still view goes idle after 16 identical frames, not 8.
6. **Invalidation.** Sun, light, environment, program, material and caster changes key the relight. Cells keep a stable draw identity (`draw stable id << 16 | instance`), so materials survive changes in draw order.

### 2.2 The shared receiver lookup

`indirect_irradiance` and `indirect_radiance` now share one lookup per receiver (`gi_prepare`, called from the generated entry before `surface`; [gi_api.wgsl](../shaders/gi_api.wgsl)). ES1's specular visibility (`es_prepare`) reads the same blended record, as requested. The lookup:
- blends the two levels around a level switch, each normalized over its own bilinear weights;
- weights each corner by its agreement with the receiver's viewed vertex normal: `clamp(4 dot(n_cell, n_rx), 0, 1)`, full within about 75°, none facing away;
- fills weight that corners lack (orphans, absent or disagreeing cells) from the next coarser level, instead of renormalizing over the corners that remain;
- falls back to the second bin, then to coarser levels, then to the open environment.

All weights are fixed by geometry: no jitter and no sample-count weighting (W3, C5).

`indirect_radiance` returns ES1's occluded sky lobe plus the bounce L1 evaluated in a GGX-width lobe (`gi_lobe_band1`). ES1's sky specular occlusion is unchanged (sky only), as the ruling requires.

### 2.4 Constraints on user shaders

- **Opt-in hooks.** A program reflects bounce light only if it declares `fn scatter(s: Surface, input: SurfaceInput, wi: vec3<f32>, wo: vec3<f32>) -> vec3<f32>` (its BRDF times cos is applied by the engine; scatter returns the BRDF value) and/or `fn emission(s: Surface, input: SurfaceInput) -> vec3<f32>`.
  - A program with neither blocks light in the cache but reflects none: its surfaces are black to bounce. The standard program declares `scatter` (its own `brdf`).
  - A hook with another signature is rejected when the program is created, with a message naming both signatures.
- **One view-independent outgoing radiance per cell.**
  - `scatter` is evaluated toward the cell's normal (`wo = n`).
  - The cache's incident light is reflected through the program's hemispherical response, over 16 fixed directions.
  - Strongly view-dependent materials (mirrors, sheen at grazing angles) bounce as if seen head-on. That's exact for Lambert and an approximation otherwise.
- **`surface` runs at the cell's query point** with the receiver's material, UVs and vertex normal there, a footprint of the cell edge for texture gradients, no screen position and no camera. Programs that read camera-dependent inputs in `surface` get the cell's fixed values.
- **No lighting-model shortcut in the cache.** Only the program's own hooks decide reflected light, and cell shading binds only its own group 0 (work, outgoing radiance, draw table). A naga test asserts that nothing it reaches reads traced shading's camera or hit records. Programs stay fully programmable (AGENTS.md).
- **Composition on traced heads.** Since RT1d, traced heads' shadow visibility filter calls the traced entry's hit-record helpers. A cell module composed on such a head therefore also includes `PrimaryHit`, `primary_decode` and `primary_input` (`program.rs::traced_hit_helpers`). Cell shading never runs that filter: its light comes from the light pass.
- **Non-callers pay nothing** (section 7).

## 3. Quality (M)

**Harness.** `gpu_g2_quarry` ([src/gi_cache_tests.rs](../src/gi_cache_tests.rs)), one path per process. Each run uses ES1's registered quarry manifest (its own materials, the RLE2 sky, the sun on 4/12/40 m splits, a black backdrop, exposure −14), with Interactive shadows and traced primaries on RT, and Virtual shadows, the visibility buffer and G1c's cooked near field on non-RT. Variants:
- `off`: the default image;
- `es1`: the cache without bounce;
- `g2`: the cache with bounce and ES1;
- `es1open`: ES1's unoccluded diagnostic, the same settings in a process whose `environment_lobes.wgsl` skips sky specular occlusion.

Static captures are poses 0 and 1 after a reset and 12 frames, then TR1 (`Reconstruction::identity()`) over 8 and 64 further still frames (`tr1x8`, `tr1x64`). Motion uses U0b's pan (2 cm a frame), captured at witnesses 97, 112, 128 and 159, without and with TR1 from the cut (`pan`, `pantr1`).

**Scoring.** [scripts/m7-g2-score.py](../scripts/m7-g2-score.py) uses ES1's metric over every registered population, plus two inspection populations on the static poses:
- `crop`: Islam's crop, x 880-1120, y 480-640 of pose 0;
- `hollow`: the hollow under the central slab inside it, x 975-1030, y 555-595.

It adds IQ1's pinhole count, compares against Lumen's Epic floors (U0b's static floors at pose 0, its motion floors for the pan), and lists the comparators each population loses to.

**What the remaining pinholes are (M, RT, pose 0, table dumps and cell diagnostics in `runs/c6`-`d2`).**
- **Where.** 101 of the 106 cache-only TR1 pinholes read their own level (path 0) with most of the bilinear weight (median 0.85).
  - The corner cells' skies are near zero: sky over open ≤ 0.01, against 0.3-0.9 for the neighbours' lookups.
  - The pixels sit at crevice mouths, for example the gap between the orange block and the scanned rock right of the central slab.
- **Why the cells are dark.** Their query points lie on the visible surface (0.02 edges off the pixel's plane, 2-3 cm across it at levels 4-5), facing the same way.
  - All 64 of their directions are known, and 0-1 are open.
  - 40-80% of the blocked rays hit back faces: the rock is an open scan shell, and the gap under its skirt is roofed by its inside, 20-28 cm away. The hit cells there have no sky, no sun and an outgoing luminance of about 4, against about 770 for open lit cells.
  - Raising the ray origin offset to the reference's opened none of them, so they are not self-intersections.
- **What the reference says.** The pixel at the mouth is bright: radiance 3,641 against 3,034 for the cache-off image, with only 29 of direct sun. The cell points, a few centimetres inside the roofed gap, really are dark.
- **Conclusion.** The cache's spatial resolution (cells of 3-6 cm at these distances) can't resolve the visibility gradient across a crevice mouth. The lookup assigns the interior's darkness to the mouth.
- **ES1 makes it visible.** ES1's unoccluded diagnostic (no bounce, sky specular unoccluded) leaves only 22 TR1 pinholes, fewer than cache-off's 40. The cache with ES1's occluded specular but no bounce leaves 173. Bounce brings that to 103, but bounce at the mouth is also underestimated (below). With the diffuse sky, the specular sky and the bounce all dark, a mouth pixel goes black.
- **Not done.** The fixes that would close this are a finer level near strong visibility gradients (more cells), or a receiver-side visibility test between pixel and query point (a ray per pixel on RT; non-RT shading has no ray source). Neither fit this unit. The miss is recorded.

**Bounce in enclosed regions is too low (M; the same cause as the hollow).** In the hollow under the central slab the reference is lit mostly by bounce (its depth-2 image holds 3.5% direct sky). There, and in the roofed gaps above:
- the cells' bounce is about a tenth of the reference's;
- the hit cells inside an enclosure see neither sky nor sun, so the multi-bounce chain (primary → secondary → tertiary cells) starts from near-zero values;
- the light that enters through openings narrower than a cell isn't represented.

ES1's unoccluded diagnostic scores better than G2 on the hollow population (9.89/16.22 against 13.52/26.74 at RT tr1x64) for the same reason: its unoccluded sky specular partly stands in for the missing bounce. The hollow's bright specks (open-sky fallbacks) are gone, and G2 beats the cache-off image there (22.84/31.08). The mottling that remains is this shortfall.

- **Tables.**
  - RT: three of four tables are word-identical; the fourth (second process, second repetition) differs in 1,872 of 165k valid slots.
  - Non-RT: 2,202-10,142 slots differ between repetitions and processes.
  - Where they differ: 99.9% of the slots are gather-made (secondary) cells, most with the retry bit, and the difference is in the bounce scale word at ulp level (median 1e-7 relative, p99 2e-4).
  - Origin: one slot per table differs entirely, an insert-placement race between gather requests for keys in the same bucket. Its neighbours' retry gathers carry the difference on at ulp level.
  - Pixel-read (primary) cells are unaffected, which is why the images agree.
  - This traces the kind of cross-process table difference G1d saw on RT (0.24%). It doesn't reach the image.
- **Cache off identical to main.** ES1's quarry harness (`gpu_es1_quarry`, static, cache off, poses 0 and 1) on this branch's binary and on main `2a7287d`: byte-identical on both paths (`runs/identity`). The cache-on captures differ, as intended.
