# Renderlab's open problems: what to learn from other renderers

2026-11-06. Research base: `bc126f7e321326180c07891ca09bbbddcebf29f8` on `t3/competitor-weakness-tests`. Research only: no renderer edits, builds, GPU runs, windows, captures, new measurements or restricted-source reading. Islam's clarification makes **our weaknesses and possible remedies the primary subject**. Competitor limitations and matched test scenes follow in the smaller second part.

The most useful immediate reuse is **obvhs/tray_racing for N0**, **Bevy's culled visibility buffer for N1b if needed**, and **small environment-shader compositions for reload latency**. Motion AA has the largest unresolved quality gap: no source examined demonstrates our film gate within the extra 0.21 ms budget. Competitor artifacts are workloads to investigate, not evidence that Renderlab already wins.

## 1. Our ten open problems and concrete things to learn

### P1. Film-quality AA in motion: sample integration costs too much

**Our gap (M/B).** Resampled T1 history leaks across shadow boundaries; T2 records preserve much more detail but are expensive. T2b's deterministic append/sort scatter already reproduced the gather's images and records and still cost **4.251 ms at K=8**, against **0.21 ms extra** over Interactive to reach the **1.075 ms stop**. That stop is separate from the **0.80 ms 2x-VSM floor**. T2 also has transient, bias and hidden-record failures. The later probe found a two-surface sliver misclassified as solid and receiver-origin signatures; it did not establish a T2 fix or clear the gate. [Motion design](../m6-motion-aa.md), [GPU result, sections 9.1-9.2](../m6-motion-aa-gpu.md#9-follow-up-the-outliers-and-the-scatter-price).

| How others address it | Cost and quality trade-off | Licence / take |
|---|---|---|
| **TSR** accumulates/reprojects image history; Epic/Cinematic use 200% history dimensions to counter reprojection blur. **F:** Epic documents both the blur problem and its higher-resolution remedy. | History-update work is **4x**, not the entire frame. Epic claims this removes reprojection blur; our step-model objection does **not** measure full TSR. Exact quarry shadow-edge behavior with TSR remains unmeasured. | Public docs/talks only; Unreal EULA code is not a take. [TSR][tsr] |
| **ATAA** adds fresh supersampling where TAA fails, with a compact list of ray workloads. | **A:** compaction improves its UE4/RTX 2070-2080/1080p prototype by **13-29%**. Most pixels still use TAA; its result does not certify our solid-pixel or 0.21 ms gate. | Reimplement the paper's allocator/compaction; do not transplant UE code. [ATAA chapter][ataa] |
| **Area ReSTIR** carries subpixel path coordinates and matches translated footprints to 2x2 previous pixels. | **A:** 1080p/RTX 4090 teaser: 14.0 ms versus ReSTIR PT 9.5 ms; different workloads in other figures. It is millisecond-scale path tracing, not a cheap AA pass. | Algorithm study only: repository BSD notice conflicts with proprietary module headers. [Paper/project][area], [licence audit](reuse-temporal-denoise.md#13-area-restir-sub-pixel-point-reuse-with-a-22-window) |
| **Filament** filters geometric-normal variance into specular roughness. **Cycles** spends independent fresh paths and uses adaptive sampling. | Filtering targets specular aliasing, not silhouette coverage or shadow visibility; excess roughness is a documented risk. Cycles spends more samples on difficult pixels; no real-time cost guarantee. | Filament Apache-2.0; standalone Cycles Apache-2.0, Blender application GPL. [Filament controls][filament-materials], [Cycles sampling][cycles-sampling], [precision/licence survey](reuse-engines-precision.md) |

**Try/study next (I).** First replay saved subpixel samples to split irreducible error into coverage, BRDF bandwidth and shadow changes. Evaluate Filament-style specular integration **inside an opt-in standard material**, against the original high-sample program output; retain only if all relevant populations improve without bias. Separately price ATAA-style **compacted fresh rays** on the existing discontinuity/revealed-pixel list, on both backends. This may improve a component; it is not a proposed complete 0.21 ms solution. A novel point-reuse design needs a new cost model before another GPU implementation.

**Do not restart the old scatter recommendation.** The temporal survey's optimistic scatter estimate predates T2b. A u64 minimum-per-stratum design changes sample selection and is not identical to T2b's append/sort, but neither correctness nor a budget win follows. Require a saved-buffer proof and a component bound first. Preserve F1-F10, especially F2's revealed-pixel test, F5 hidden weights and F8. **Scene S2** below is the minimal witness. [Latest scatter evidence](../m6-motion-aa-gpu.md), [frozen film gates](../m6-temporal-primaries.md#7-level-f-gate-for-t1).

### P2. Interactive still misses the complete-frame 0.80 ms floor

**Our gap (B/M).** Current brief: **0.91 ms versus 0.80 ms**. Historical quiet results are 0.82-0.87 ms and a contended T1 run measured about 0.91 ms; these are not interchangeable current measurements. At 0.91 ms we need **0.11 ms / 12.1%** less complete-frame time (I, arithmetic). Historical shading is about 0.396 ms, primary/fixed work about 0.30 ms, and tone about 0.049 ms. [Non-RT budgets](../non-rt-path.md#per-pass-budgets), [Interactive climb](../m6-interactive-climb.md), [contended temporal observations](../m6-temporal-gpu.md).

**How others solve similar costs (F/A).** Nanite separates visibility from material evaluation and groups material work; its 2021 demo reports approximately **2.5 ms visibility and 2 ms material work**, around 2496x1404 internal to 4K, not a transferable Renderlab budget. Bevy's author doubled depth/material resolve performance by skipping background work and replaced an expensive R64 texture clear with a compute clear. id Tech 6's 2016 talk describes clustered forward lighting, shared classifications, particle-light caching and async post. These avoid redundant work or improve coherence; neither a cheap GI subtotal nor an async sum proves a cheaper frame. [Nanite talk][nanite-talk], [Bevy author report][bevy-meshlet-post], [id Tech 6][idtech], [verified cost contexts](industry-rendering-costs.md).

**Cost/quality and licence.** Grouping, empty-tile rejection and exact algebraic reuse can preserve output, but extra compaction/dispatch has overhead; Renderlab already shades hit tiles and fuses Interactive tracing. Copy Bevy selectively under MIT/Apache; study id/Epic talks as algorithms only. Filament's preintegrated IBL is Apache-2.0 but split-sum approximations have visible error; it cannot replace RLE2 merely to save time. [Current frame structure](../m6-primary-ray-production.md), [Filament approximations][filament], [licence survey](reuse-engines-precision.md).

**Try next (I).** Count redundant environment-level evaluations and repeated material/descriptor work in the existing shader, then retain one small byte-equivalent composition/hoist. The reload study's two-level environment probe is evidence of **compile savings only**, not runtime savings. If another pass or compaction costs more than the work avoided, reject it. Price one retained variant in one paired process against the current whole frame; rerun Level B/F and both standard/toon programs only for the kept change. Savings on software traversal or N1b might transfer to hardware primaries, but do not budget them twice. **S1/S3** test unchanged contact quality while pursuing F9 and the 2x floor.

### P3. Shader edit-to-image misses 100 ms

**Our gap (M).** Standard-program history: about **1 s driver compile per pipeline**, with environment inlining about 80% of cost; flat programs compile in **22-58 ms**. Toon quarry observations are **236.6 ms raster / 391.3 ms traced save-to-completed-offscreen-image**, single samples excluding watcher/presentation. They are different shaders/methods, not a new 1 s measurement. [Reload profile](../fast-shader-reload.md), [toon latency](../programmable-shading-phase-2.md#edit-latency).

**Others' solution (F).** Godot 4.4+ prepares an ubershader and compiles specialized pipelines in the background; its shader baker produces intermediate code, not hardware-specific final pipelines. Cycles' Metal backend similarly starts with a generic kernel and specializes in the background. Bevy queues pipeline creation asynchronously, but the surveyed implementation has no driver pipeline cache and loses the old pipeline during reload. Renderlab already has on-demand sets, a session cache and last-good retention. [Godot pipeline documentation][godot-compile], [Cycles 3.5 kernel specialization][cycles-light-tree], [Bevy comparison](reuse-engines-precision.md#c-fast-shader-iteration).

**Cost/quality and licence.** Async work removes a stall but does not shorten save-to-new-image. An ubershader can cover known engine switches, not arbitrary new user source. Godot/Bevy are permissive; the architectural pattern matters more than copying their caches. Vulkan exposes a disable-optimization creation flag, but the survey found no wgpu 30 pipeline flag/library/shader-object route. Neither fast compilation nor pixel equivalence under that flag is established. [Vulkan flags][vk-pipeline-flags], [wgpu boundary audit](reuse-engines-precision.md#c-fast-shader-iteration).

**Try next (I).** First simplify environment composition without changing arithmetic: the existing two-level probe was **37-42% faster to compile and HDR-byte-identical**. Then investigate an engine-owned environment stage or reusable input that leaves arbitrary `shade()` semantics intact; record the contract before splitting it. A small driver-only unoptimized/optimized compile probe is worthwhile before any wgpu-hal patch. Measure unique uncached edits, validation/rejection, hot cached reverts and startup separately; keep last-good presentation. **S8** below is a developer-latency fixture, not a frame-quality win.

### P4. Non-RT primaries and shadows need a credible traversal baseline

**Our gap (M/I).** N0/N1 are plans on this base, not a verified software renderer. The 2-5x per-ray estimate is unmeasured here; primaries may exceed **0.40 ms**. Quality equality is the intended shared-estimator contract and still requires intersection/rounding validation. [Non-RT plan](../non-rt-path.md).

**Others' solution (F/A).** obvhs offers Rust PLOC building, compressed 8-wide 80-byte nodes and a CPU oracle. tray_racing supplies wgpu-driven HLSL software traversal and a hardware comparison. Its published chart, read in the detailed survey, reports **2.75 ms software / 0.51 ms hardware = 5.4x** on RTX 4070 Ti, 1080p, one primary plus one unbounded closest-hit AO ray, eight-scene average. Sun any-hit rays, WGSL compilation and our scene are different. Bevy uses two-pass HZB culling and software/hardware raster visibility; strolle is a Rust/wgpu software-ray GI reference. [obvhs][obvhs], [tray_racing][tray], [chart/context audit](reuse-non-rt.md#31-published-software-versus-hardware-evidence), [Bevy meshlets][bevy-meshlet-post], [strolle][strolle].

**Cost/quality and licence.** obvhs/tray/Bevy are MIT-or-Apache; strolle MIT. The kernel port must preserve our watertight triangle test, interval and hit ABI; the reference kernels do not automatically do so. Quantization must expand bounds conservatively. Bevy's surveyed software raster lacks fixed-point/top-left rules and near clipping; classify genuine edge ties and implement clipping/fill correctness rather than accepting unexplained mismatches. HLSL-to-WGSL stacks/subgroups can change throughput. strolle documents weak high-resolution performance; it is a learning reference, not evidence of 1080p film-quality speed. [N0/N1b requirements](../non-rt-path.md#units-and-stop-rules), [detailed code/licence findings](reuse-non-rt.md).

**Try next (I).** Use obvhs for N0's builder/oracle instead of writing another builder; first check index-only leaves and peak memory. Port one closest-hit and one early-out shadow loop from tray_racing. Replay saved rays and compare to brute force before GPU pricing. If primaries exceed 0.40 ms, reuse Bevy's culling/visibility pattern with existing meshoptimizer clusters at **finest detail**, not LOD. Test on a device created without RT features as required by N1. The 5.4x ratio is a warning, never a Renderlab result. **S1/S3** exercise common images; actual low-L2 non-RT hardware remains N3.

### P5. Soft shadows cover one sun, not point/spot sources

**Our gap (B/M).** The sampled soft estimator supports one directional disk; M6 has hard point/spot shadows, and M5 has local-light shading, but these are not complete soft local-light support. [M6 shadows](../m6-shadows.md), [non-RT supported scope](../non-rt-path.md#units-and-stop-rules).

**Others' solution (F/A).** HDRP samples finite-radius local sources and denoises the result: its docs explicitly say sample cost grows linearly and lower samples require more filtering. MegaLights uses bounded stochastic direct-light work, importance guiding and a shared denoiser rather than enumerating every light. **A:** its SIGGRAPH 2025 example is **5.51 ms, 941 area lights, PS5, 1080p, 1 spp**. The talk uses weighted reservoir sampling without ReSTIR reuse, citing its constant cost/correlation. Cycles' light tree increases per-sample work to reduce many-light variance. [HDRP shadows][hdrp-shadows], [MegaLights talk][mega-talk], [Cycles 3.5 light sampling][cycles-light-tree].

**Cost/quality and licence.** Unreal/HDRP code is not reusable here; public algorithms are study material. Bevy light presampling and strolle ReSTIR are permissive references, but their BRDF target functions and patent questions need separate treatment. A fixed sample budget bounds work, not missing-light/ghost error; exhaustive finite-source rays give a simple oracle but scale with light count. [Engine survey](reuse-engines-precision.md), [sampling/patent audit](reuse-temporal-denoise.md#3-sampling-efficiency).

**Try next (I).** Begin with one explicitly defined spherical point source and a sphere-source spotlight with angular falloff. Sample finite endpoints with correct PDFs and world-space `tmax`, so geometry behind the light cannot shadow it. Share source samples/estimators across ray backends; evaluate each sample's direction in the user program rather than multiplying an arbitrary central-light BRDF by an average visibility. Only after the single-source oracle passes, study cluster light lists and MegaLights-style initial guiding; retain a cheap exhaustive control at 1/8/32 lights. **S7** checks contact-hardening, finite endpoints, light cones and source overlap. Rectangular emitters are a separate follow-up.

### P7. Edge and primary-hit precision adds roughly 3-5%

**Our gap (B/M).** The 3-5% current cost is the brief's estimate; the detailed survey explicitly did not find its full measurement record. Recorded witnesses have distinct real crossings only **0.24-3.5 micrometres apart**, with the hardware choosing the farther triangle. This differs from a shared-edge crack and from secondary-ray self-intersection. [Precision survey](reuse-engines-precision.md#b-edge-hit-and-self-intersection-robustness), [determinism evidence](../determinism.md).

**Others' solution (F).** NVIDIA bounds reconstruction/transform error before spawning rays. pbrt uses error intervals and a watertight intersection with rare double-precision fallback. Standalone Cycles normally starts at the exact surface and excludes the source primitive; it applies an offset after a failed self-triangle retest. None guarantees sub-ulp ordering between distinct hardware hits. [NVIDIA analysis][safe-spawn], [pbrt geometry arithmetic][pbrt-errors], [DXR intersection contract][dxr], [Vulkan traversal][vk-rays], [Cycles policy audit](reuse-engines-precision.md#b2-published-spawn-point-and-offset-methods).

**Cost/quality and licence.** NVIDIA's sample is **BSD-3**, separate from the restricted RTX SDKs. pbrt and standalone Cycles are Apache-2.0. Always forcing any-hit adds cost and does not remove adjacent-triangle problems. A tighter offset bound shrinks a band; it does not prove safety against a neighboring concave plane. GPU-specific error constants and WGSL arithmetic need validation. [Sample licence][safe-spawn-license], [NVIDIA explanation][safe-spawn], [fold analysis](reuse-engines-precision.md#b3-does-pbrt-v4s-bound-remove-the-concave-fold-false-shadow).

**Try next (I).** Replay the three saved witnesses with a **back-facing, near-edge-only front-face retrace**, bounded around the committed distance, before replacing adjacency work. All three surveyed farther hits were back-facing; this is our proposed shortcut, not a published general solution. Keep genuine open-shell back views and mirrored transforms. Separately test a direction-only fold-safe offset at the adopted magnitude; do not change the receiver policy as a performance tweak. **S1's fold/open-shell lane** plus existing witnesses tests geometry correctness; quantify the rare-path rate, total time, Level A differences and film-solid failures. Software needs the same policy with its own intersection oracle.

### P8. Driver arithmetic changes and compensated residual folding

**Our gap (M/B).** The standalone arithmetic reproducer showed changing rays from unchanged inputs/SPIR-V. Explicit FMA in camera-ray construction preserved accepted bytes across the recorded repeats. A raw-SPIR-V `NoContraction` control stopped drift but changed baseline bits. The broader residual-folding problem is current brief/survey evidence, not proof that the camera fix solves every kernel. [Determinism investigation](../determinism.md), [arithmetic survey](reuse-engines-precision.md#b5-arithmetic-notes-relevant-to-determinism).

**Others' solution/contract (F).** HLSL `precise`/SPIR-V `NoContraction` protects sensitive arithmetic in NVIDIA's error-bound method. WGSL permits transformations and has no equivalent source qualifier; Vulkan's default expression rules allow algebraic rearrangement without the relevant controls. Vulkan does not promise pixel equality across implementations, and invariance has side-effect qualifications. No public engine report examined supplies a cheap universal fix or cross-vendor bit identity. [WGSL floating evaluation][wgsl], [Vulkan SPIR-V arithmetic][vk-floats], [invariance][vk-invariance], [NVIDIA method][safe-spawn].

**Cost/quality and licence.** Specifications are study material; no vendor code is required. The survey found wgpu shader passthrough for a diagnostic SPIR-V path, not a portable WGSL strictness switch. Explicit FMA was measured as a bounded local fix, not a specification-wide determinism guarantee. Strict arithmetic may alter output and throughput; its cost is unknown here. [wgpu/naga audit](reuse-engines-precision.md#b5-arithmetic-notes-relevant-to-determinism).

**Try next (I).** Keep a small saved-input arithmetic corpus with expected bit patterns, including cancellation residuals, transforms and edge-order comparisons. Compare original, explicit-FMA and strict diagnostic SPIR-V; preserve intentional FMA while constraining the compensated steps. Hash outputs on first use, after warmup, after a reload/revert and camera return; ordinary image tests can miss first-use drift. For software traversal, use robust integer/expansion tie decisions only in the ambiguous predicate if affordable; WGSL floating expansions without contraction control are not automatically exact. Report within-device F8 separately from cross-vendor geometric agreement. This is a CPU/spec investigation now; any future GPU pricing waits for authorization/availability.

### P9. Streaming/LOD and full-detail tracing do not yet form one scalable path

**Our gap (M).** The quarry fits full-resident geometry, but hardware AS allocator attribution is approximately **912 MB**, plus separately reported scratch; this is not measured physical VRAM residency. Full-detail tracing is not arbitrary-world streaming. A coarse primary cut against full-detail casters displaces the receiver relative to its own occluder geometry. [AS accounting](../combined-rendering-cost.md#startup-and-as-accounting), [non-RT LOD decision](../non-rt-path.md#decisions), [scan limits](../scan-results.md).

**Others' solution (F/A).** Nanite uses cluster hierarchies, compressed data and view-driven streaming; Bevy/meshoptimizer provide reusable meshlet clustering/culling infrastructure. Epic documents the trade between simplified RT proxies and detailed raster geometry; full Nanite RT detail raises memory/build cost and changing cuts can hitch. obvhs compresses wide nodes; software per-cluster BVHs could avoid a hardware cluster-AS API dependency. **No** cited measurement establishes our expected 0.15-0.35 GB software BVH footprint; it remains the non-RT plan's estimate. [Nanite overview][nanite], [MegaLights RT-scene section][mega], [obvhs][obvhs], [reuse audit](reuse-non-rt.md).

**Cost/quality and licence.** Bevy MIT/Apache, meshoptimizer MIT and obvhs MIT/Apache are copy/dependency candidates; Epic source is excluded. Keep full detail for in-memory scenes. For out-of-core content, consistent primary/shadow cuts avoid one mismatch but **do not prove the simplification preserves film-reference contacts or reflected detail**. CPU selection, residency debt, AS build/refit and memory are part of cost. [Existing streaming targets](../milestones-5-6.md#parallel-m4-work), [non-RT later scope](../non-rt-path.md#units-and-stop-rules).

**Try next (I).** Study a small per-cluster software BVH plus a top level over the selected cut, with explicit cut-generation ownership and old resources retained until all in-flight consumers retire. Reuse existing cooked hierarchy rather than inventing a second LOD tree. CPU-replay a small corridor's cut/residency sequence before tracing it. Price build/update spikes separately from warm traversal and shader iteration. Compare full-detail film reference, cut-consistent reference and final candidate as distinct diagnostics; the simplified reference never replaces the film gate. **S6/S9** test overlap, missing detail, fast turns and newly resident casters on both paths.

### P10. Exact visibility is 8-10 ms and is not film-quality AA

**Our gap (M).** Exact traces 256 shadow directions per pixel-centre receiver, around **7.6-10 ms** depending on GPU state. It does not integrate the pixel footprint, so reducing Exact's time alone does not solve P1. Measured 25 cm-capped rays keep **0.917** of full traversal cost: a far-field map cannot plausibly remove most hardware cost. [Non-RT budgets](../non-rt-path.md), [fresh-compute limiter](../m6-fresh-compute-cost.md), [film gap](../m6-film-gap.md).

**Others' solution (F/A).** Cycles allocates samples adaptively to convergence; this reduces expected variance work, not a worst-case exact-ray bound. MegaLights/denoisers lower fresh sample count with bias/lag risk. Tiled per-triangle soft-shadow volumes amortize caster processing across a **208-cell disk mask**. **A:** its wgpu/RTX 4070/~13M-triangle/4K example reports roughly **5 ms evaluation + 1.3 ms gather**, highly content/sun-angle dependent. This is a different quadrature from our 256 directions. [Cycles sampling][cycles-sampling], [MegaLights][mega], [shadow-volume article][soft-volumes], [survey timings/assumptions](reuse-non-rt.md#21-tiled-per-triangle-soft-shadow-volumes-kalbertodt-reimplement-only-worth-a-probe).

**Cost/quality and licence.** The volume gist has no licence in the survey: reimplement the article, copy no shader. Its light-facing cull assumes closed geometry and is unsafe on open quarry scans. FTIZB is a useful exact hard-shadow alternative, but its published GTX 980/1080p/12.3M-triangle costs are **20.0 ms at 1 spp / 32.2 ms at 32 spp**; no modern speed extrapolation justifies another implementation. [FTIZB paper][ftizb], [non-RT survey](reuse-non-rt.md#2-non-rt-shadows).

**Try next (I).** First build a tiny **CPU** disk-mask/triangle-union replay on a closed wedge and an open scan-style shell; check caster orientation and overlapping occluders before GPU work. To count as an Exact optimization it must evaluate the frozen **same 256 directions** and receiver intervals with identical visibility bits. The article's 208-sample scheme would instead be a new candidate, gated independently against the unchanged film reference. Try traversal coherence/node reuse only with those same rays; a lower sample count or denoised image is not an Exact speedup. **S1/S7** provide the smallest overlap/penumbra witnesses.

### What the existing Unreal numbers actually establish

**M:** native UE 5.7.4, RTX 4070 SUPER, 1920x1080, no AA/reconstruction, full 13,034,903 HRT triangles: pose-0 partial visibility RMS is **0.369477 VSM**, **0.390556 HRT 1-spp denoised**, **0.198386 HRT 256 raw**, versus production **0.006532** on common partial receivers. Removing VSM normal bias gives **0.287303**, still different. These are strong reproducible policy/sampling witnesses, **not complete film-image scores**. The reference uses Renderlab's geometric receiver policy; source-only geometry leaves **20/28/20 missing receivers**, including one partial per pose. Native-receiver references and full radiance/film scoring must stay separate from the frozen comparison. [Masks, populations and limits](../unreal-native-shadow-mask.md).

**M:** fresh static packaged medians were **1.59650 ms VSM**, **2.14975 ms HRT 1 spp**, and **26.25730 ms HRT 256 raw**; epsilon-zero raw HRT had equal masks at **16.58935 ms**. These single-process costs do not establish motion or replicated 2x wins. In particular the independently warmed return pose is **not** contiguous return-history evidence. Existing 31% lost-contact / 2,191-flip summaries come from an earlier Level B setup and are not substitutes for this later full-detail native-mask profile. [Fresh timing/limits](../unreal-native-shadow-mask.md#fresh-packaged-timing-pose-0), [older Level B](../m6-level-b.md).

**Excluded as current weaknesses:** UE-290280's narrow-spot VSM artifact is marked **fixed, target 5.7**; test it only if it reproduces in our exact build. Likewise, old Solari 0.19 shadow lag has an explicit 0.20 previous-TLAS mitigation. No current Frostbite or id Tech regression is inferred from an older talk. [Epic issue][ue-fixed], [Solari update][solari].

## 3. Reproducible paired scenes

### Shared capture and scoring contract

All following specifications are **I: proposed fixtures**, not existing executable harnesses. The geometry/camera/light tracks can be authored in both Renderlab and the private UE 5.7 project; missing Renderlab features are marked as prerequisites. No scene is claimed runnable today on an unimplemented software backend. Build **S1-S3 first** because current directional-light paths can exercise them without adding GI or local soft-shadow features.

- Use generated, redistributable geometry in metres, Y up, linear material values. Export one mesh/placement manifest and one timestamped camera/light track to both engines; UE gets the existing source-to-UE basis and centimetre conversion. Hash both inputs and effective readbacks. Begin with 640x360 crops for diagnosis, then retained comparisons at **1920x1080**, fixed exposure and 60 Hz simulation.
- Match camera matrices/FOV, source size/intensity, textures/filtering, normals, double-sided/thin semantics, shutter and enabled effects. A directional angular radius **0.04 rad** means UE Source Angle **4.583662361 degrees diameter**; verify the render proxy, not just UObject values. Do not fit source angle or bias to absorb image error. [Existing mapping and stale-proxy lesson](../unreal-native-shadow-mask.md#native-mask-receivers-and-light-readback).
- **Reference:** independent high-sample pixel-footprint and light integration at every scored time, no reconstruction/denoising. Reuse existing valid references; for new geometry cross-check with Cycles/pbrt or an independent integrator and replicate until uncertainty is below the compared error. Match zero shutter for crisp-motion sequences first; a separate finite-shutter track must use the same interval everywhere. Receiver-policy diagnostics do not replace final-film radiance. Never label a 256-direction pixel-centre image the film reference.
- **UE candidate matrix:** retain a high-quality native VSM/no-HW-RT path and full-detail HRT path; add Lumen SW/HW only for GI/reflection scenes. For S2 keep native-resolution TSR Epic/200% history and price its actual frame; report a tuned quality-matched configuration if one passes. AA-off controls isolate shadows but cannot win the film gate. MegaLights uses separately recorded supported settings. Feature toggles are effective runtime readbacks, not assumed defaults.
- **Renderlab matrix:** hardware and software using the same scene, estimator and program; a no-RT-feature device proves software independence. Until N0/N1, software is an explicit missing result. No missing GI, reflections, transparency or local shadows may be silently disabled in a matched-effect comparison.
- **Metrics:** linear HDR error plus registered/+4-stop display error, per-population RMS/p99/max/signed mean, contact recall/false shadows, silhouette coverage and worst frame. Sequence metrics include response age, lag, residual flicker and full-frame flipbooks. [Level A](../m6-reduced-ray-gate.md), [Level B U/S checks](../m6-level-b.md), [F1-F10](../m6-temporal-primaries.md#7-level-f-gate-for-t1) retain their frozen thresholds; new scene populations and GI/reflection measures need registration before scoring, not retroactive thresholds.
- **Speed:** measure complete frame GPU critical path and CPU service/pacing, plus attribution for traversal, cache/AS work and streaming. Record source/binary hashes, hardware/driver, resolution, reconstruction, quality and contention. One process loads a fixture once and runs all variants, starting with **8 paired repeats**, growing only for unresolved variance. Hash comparison before full readback. Development latency and reference/scoring time are separate from rendering. This note ran none of it. [Fast verification policy](../../AGENTS.md).

### First three scenes

**S1 — Contact, fold and stacked-occluder bench (first).** A 2x2 m grey floor (two triangles), a 1 m rail carrying opaque strips **1/5/20 mm wide**, placed **0.2/1/5 mm above** it, and two tilted 20x20 cm plates at heights **2/10 cm**, overlapping in sun projection. Add one two-face 60-degree concave crease, its convex control, and a double-sided open shell; keep these in separate labeled lanes. No normal map, roughness 0.6, metallic 0, fixed exposure; black world plus matched sun for the shadow diagnostic, then the same constant environment for final radiance. Under 1,000 triangles.

Camera eye `(1.8,1.2,2.4)`, target `(0,0.05,0)`, vertical FOV 45 degrees. Sun elevation 20 degrees, azimuth 35 degrees, radius 0.04 rad. Sequence: hold **32 frames**, dolly eye and target **0.5 m backward over 64**, hold 32, return over 64, hold 32; fixed projection. A separate static pair changes only distance to test camera-scaled contact loss. Inspect all lanes and silhouettes, not one average. Primary metrics: contact recall and umbra-to-lit flips versus film, signed shadow-edge displacement, partial visibility RMS and final HDR/display error. **Strengthens P2/P7/P10, Level A/B and F1/F3/F4/F10.** Existing hardware directional features suffice; software needs N1. Multiple depth layers test rank 5 without local-light work.

**S2 — Slivers, shading frequency and a moving shadow step (second).** Reuse S1's floor; add a diagonal 0.5 m bar and opaque double-sided wires whose projected widths at the starting pose are **0.25/0.5/1/2 px**, plus a 20 cm faceted glossy patch (roughness 0.1) and a diffuse control. Use a tilted blocker to put a hard shadow edge across the diffuse interior. Hard-source run isolates footprint integration; repeat with the S1 disk. No alpha blending in the first version, so transparency does not confound coverage. Under 5,000 triangles; generate widths from the initial camera projection and record them.

Camera `(0,1,3)`, looking at `(0,0.2,0)`, vertical FOV 45 degrees. Translate both eye and target horizontally at **0.25 projected px/frame** on the floor for 64 frames, hold 32, return 64. For a plane at depth z and vertical focal length f in pixels, choose translation `0.25*z/f`; record the resulting full camera matrices and verify the floor's displacement in both engines. A second pass uses 0.5 and 1 px/frame; do not treat the favorable 0.5 px alignment of a 200% history as general success. Move a foreground plate sideways to reveal background during frames 16-32. A separate fixed-camera track moves only the shadow edge.

Compare actual per-time film integrals to native TSR and Renderlab candidates. Measure F1 leakage on verified **single-surface** solids, F2 error at ages 0/1/3/7, F3 bias, F4 missed/false shadows, F5 trail/hidden weight, F6 residual flicker, F8 first-use/reload/return identity, and edge-spread width as a diagnostic. Use both a standard and a simple toon program; geometry hit records must stay style-independent. **Strengthens P1/P8 and all motion gates. Current Renderlab motion is a recorded likely failure, not the expected winner.**

**S3 — Static, smoothly moving and jumping sun (third).** A 4x4 m procedural rough slab: **512x512 cells / 524,288 triangles**, height from fixed seeded smooth noise plus 1-5 mm ridges, and 64 small opaque block pillars. This stays far smaller than the quarry and cooks quickly; reduce to 128x128 for first correctness debugging. Exact same finest triangles are the in-memory Renderlab baseline. UE Nanite detail is recorded rather than silently assuming raster/shadow selections match.

Fixed camera `(4,3,5)`, target `(0,0,0)`, vertical FOV 50 degrees. Sun elevation 15 degrees, radius 0.04 rad, initial azimuth 35 degrees. Track: static 32 frames, rotate azimuth **0.25 degrees/s for 128**, hold 32; repeat at **1 degree/s**, then an isolated **5-degree jump** and 32-frame recovery. Same timestamps, no time-quantized sun or frozen stale shadows. Measure invalidated/rendered VSM pages, shadow-depth/projection cost, total GPU/CPU p50/p95/p99 and film lag/error on every phase. Ray cost may change with angle, so record it too. **Strengthens P2/P4, F3/F5/F6/F9/F10 and moving-workload 2x evidence.** No guarantee that this small scene makes page generation the bottleneck; escalate to the retained quarry only if the targeted witness warrants it.
